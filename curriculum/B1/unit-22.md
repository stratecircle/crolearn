# B1 Unit 22 — Kultura i povijest (Croatian culture & history) · Futur II; kad / ako / čim / dok

## Overview

The unit that stops the learner saying the most natural thing in the world.

*When I arrive, I'll call you.* English puts a present in the first half and a future in the second, and every learner of Croatian reaches for **Futur I** in the time clause because that is where the future actually is: *\*Kad **ću doći**, javit ću ti.* ❌ It is wrong, it is wrong every single time, and it is the only thing this unit is really about.

**In a time clause after `kad`, `čim`, `dok` or `ako` that points at the future, Croatian bans Futur I.** What goes there instead is decided by **the aspect of the verb**, and the learner does not get to choose:

> **Perfective verb → the PRESENT.** *Kad **dođem**, javit ću ti.* · *Čim **stignem**, kupit ću ulaznicu.*
> **Imperfective verb → FUTUR II** (*budem* + the l-participle). *Dok **budem čekao**, čitat ću knjigu.*

That is the whole grammar of the unit, and the second half of it is what makes it hard: **the aspect chooses, not you.** A learner who reaches for *budem* every time will produce *kad budem došao* — which is not wrong, only heavy — and a learner who reaches for the present every time will produce *\*dok čekam, čitat ću*, which reports something happening now.

Three things make this teachable exactly here. **Futur II is the last tense in the language** and needs the l-participle, which the learner has owned since the perfekt in A1. **Aspect** was Unit 17's whole subject and has been running underneath everything since; this unit is where it finally *decides* something rather than describing it. And **`kad`, `ako` and `dok` are all carded** — `kad` and `ako` at U18 L3, `dok` at U17 L4 — so only `čim` is new, and the unit is about what those words now demand rather than about the words themselves.

L1 goes back for **past narration with aspect**, deliberately, because the fork the unit turns on is the same fork U17 taught and the learner needs it warm before it starts governing a tense they have never met. L2 builds **Futur II** and puts it in `kad`-clauses. L3 adds **`čim`**, the **`dok` / `dok ne`** pair whose `ne` translates to nothing at all, and the `ako` split. L4 practises the whole system in **biography**, where time clauses are the natural grammar of a life. L5 sorts it, walks the wall of ten traps, and writes.

And there is **one licensed exception**, which the unit meets in L5 and which lands precisely because Unit 21 just happened: *Pitao me **kad ću doći**.* ✅ That `kad` is not a time conjunction at all — it is Unit 21's embedded question word, and the clause **is** the thing being asked rather than naming the time of anything else. The test is never the word; it is what the clause is doing.

Croatian culture and history carry the unit because a plan is a stack of time clauses and nothing else — *when we arrive we'll buy tickets, as soon as we've got them we'll go in, while we wait for Marko we'll look round the fortress* — and because a biography is the same stack pointed backwards. The story is a castle, four people, one day trip, and a plan in which every single arrangement is a time clause. Unit shape: 5 lessons + 300–500-word story + unit test + guided writing task (80–150 words, AI rubric-graded, never gates).

## Can-do objectives

By the end of Unit 22 the learner can:

1. **Make an arrangement without putting a future in the time clause**: *Kad dođem, javit ću ti.* · *Čim stignemo, kupit ćemo ulaznice.*
2. **Choose between the present and Futur II by aspect**, not by feel: *Kad **stignem**…* (perfective) ‖ *Dok **budem čekao**…* (imperfective).
3. **Form Futur II** for any verb: *budem / budeš / bude / budemo / budete / budu* + the l-participle, agreeing.
4. **Use `dok` and `dok ne` correctly**, and know that the `ne` in *dok ne* translates to nothing: *Čekat ću **dok ne dođeš**.*
5. **Split `ako` from `kad`** on certainty: *Ako bude kiše, nećemo ići* (it may not happen) ‖ *Kad budeš imao vremena, javi mi* (it will).
6. **Recognize the one `kad` that DOES take Futur I** — the embedded question from Unit 21: *Pitao me kad ću doći.*
7. **Write 80–150 words** planning a cultural route through Croatia, with at least one perfective present time clause and one Futur II.

## Lessons

### L1 — Malo povijesti (Past narration with aspect — the fork, warmed up)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new tense. The lesson re-runs **U17's aspect fork in past narration**, because the same fork is about to start governing a tense the learner has never met, and it must be warm before that happens.
  1. **Background is imperfective; events are perfective.** *Rat je **trajao** četiri godine* (a stretch) ‖ *Kralj je **osnovao** grad* (one act, finished). Historical writing is the cleanest possible showcase for this, because it is nothing but a stack of events laid over a stretch.
  2. **Some verbs are imperfective by nature and have no useful perfective at B1.** *trajati* is the model: a war does not *happen*, it *lasts*. Say this out loud, because L2's Futur II fork depends on the learner being able to look at a verb and say which kind it is without thinking.
  3. **The `kad` of the past**, which the learner already owns from U18 L3 and which behaves completely normally: *Kad sam bio mlad, dolazili smo svako ljeto.* Past `kad` takes a past. Nothing is banned, nothing is odd. It is here so that L2's ban lands as a **change**, not as a rule about the word `kad`.
  4. ⚠️ **The B2 neighbour, named on day one because history writing is full of it:** *Grad je **osnovan** u desetom stoljeću* is the **participial passive**, and it is the sentence every learner will want. This unit says *Kralj je **osnovao** grad* instead — active, with a subject — exactly as U20's L5 taught. → grammar-reference **§20 "Futur II and time clauses"** (new) + §11 aspect + §8 perfekt
- **New vocab:** L1 set (history, war and peace)
- **Required visuals:** **contrast diagram — pozadina ‖ događaj** (left: imperfective, the stretch — *rat je trajao*, *ljudi su živjeli* ‖ right: perfective, the event — *kralj je osnovao*, *rat je počeo*) · **callout — some verbs are only ever one kind** (*trajati* has no useful perfective; *osnovati* has no useful imperfective at B1 — and tomorrow this fact will decide a tense) · a century table (*u desetom stoljeću* with the ordinal + LOC) · a timeline flow for the unit's cast of historical facts
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (U17 aspect pair ×1 + U18 past `kad` ×1 + U20 «no participial passive» ×1) → Hook: "You learned aspect in Unit 17 and it has been quietly describing things ever since. ### L2 — Kad dođeš u Zagreb… (Futur II, and the ban)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the unit's headline, in two halves.
  1. **THE BAN.** In a time clause after `kad` pointing at the future, **Futur I is not allowed**. *\*Kad **ću doći**, javit ću ti* ❌ — and this is not a stylistic preference, it is simply not Croatian. The main clause keeps its Futur I; the time clause loses it.
  2. **What goes there instead is decided by ASPECT.**
     **Perfective → the PRESENT.** *Kad **dođem**, javit ću ti.* · *Kad **stignemo**, kupit ćemo ulaznice.* The present form here is not a present meaning — a perfective verb has no present meaning available, which is exactly why the slot is free for this job. (U17 taught that; this is where it pays.)
     **Imperfective → FUTUR II.** *Dok **budem čekao**, čitat ću.* · *Kad **budeš imao** vremena, javi mi.*
  3. **Futur II is built** from *budem / budeš / bude / budemo / budete / budu* + **the l-participle**, which agrees in gender and number exactly as in the perfekt: *budem **radio*** / *budem **radila*** / *budemo **radili***. The learner has had the participle since A1; the auxiliary is the only new form in the unit.
  4. **The heavy-but-legal case.** *Kad **budem došao**…* is grammatical. It is simply heavy, because *doći* is perfective and the present already does the job. Mark it as **not an error** — the only trap in the unit that costs nothing — and say why: a learner who over-uses *budem* is safe; a learner who over-uses Futur I is not.
  → grammar-reference **§20** + §9 Futur I + §11 aspect
- **New vocab:** L2 set (castles, towers, tickets)
- **Required visuals:** **callout — THE BAN** (the unit's central printable: *\*Kad ću doći* ❌ in red, *Kad dođem* ✅ beside it, with the main clause's *javit ću ti* left untouched so the contrast is visible) · **bracket diagram — the aspect fork** (top: "what kind of verb is it?" → **svršeni** → the PRESENT ‖ **nesvršeni** → **FUTUR II**) · the full Futur II conjugation table (budem + participle × 6 persons × gender) · a two-clause anatomy diagram (time clause ‖ main clause, with the two tenses labelled)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L1 aspect ×2 + U9 Futur I formation ×1) → Hook: "*When I arrive, I'll call you.* You are about to get that sentence wrong, and so does everybody — because the future is obviously in both halves, and Croatian puts it in only one." → vocab-cards (L2 set) → Teach 1: **the ban** (the callout) → Teach 2: the aspect fork + the Futur II table → Examples + TTS: *Kad dođem, javit ću ti.* · *Kad stignemo, kupit ćemo ulaznice.* · *Kad budeš imao vremena, javi mi.* · *Dok budem čekao, razgledat ću tvrđavu.* · *Kad razgledamo dvorac, idemo u kulu.* → quiz-set ×4 (two ban items, two aspect-fork items) → match (verb ↔ present or Futur II) → fill ×3 (*Kad ___ , javit ću ti. (doći, ja)* → dođem) → type ×2 (build a two-clause arrangement) → listen-type ×2 → speak (three plans for a visit) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U9's Futur I (which stays in the main clause and must not be disturbed), U17 aspect (now deciding a tense), U8's l-participle (the Futur II is built from it), U18's `kad` (same word, new demand)
- **SRS:** L2 vocab; cloze "Kad ___ , javit ću ti. (doći, ja)" → dođem; cloze "Dok ___ ___ , čitat ću. (čekati, ja)" → budem čekao; rule card "time clause + future → NEVER ___" → Futur I; form card "budem + ___" → the l-participle
- **Note:** the ban comes first and the fork second, in that order, every time. A learner who has only the fork will still write *kad ću*; a learner who has only the ban will write *kad budem* for everything, which is merely clumsy. The ban is the safety rail and the fork is the polish.

### L3 — Ako budeš učio… (čim · dok / dok ne · the ako split)

- **Grammar micro-point:** three more conjunctions, all obeying L2's rule, and one of them with a quirk.
  1. **`čim` = as soon as**, and it behaves exactly like `kad`: *Čim **stignem**, kupit ću ulaznicu.* It is almost always followed by a **perfective present**, because "as soon as" is by nature a completed trigger. New word, no new rule.
  2. **`dok` = while**, and it is the unit's natural home for **Futur II**, because "while" is by nature a stretch and stretches are imperfective: *Dok **budem čekao** Marka, razgledat ću tvrđavu.*
  3. **`dok ne` = until — and the `ne` means nothing.** *Čekat ću **dok ne dođeš**.* — I'll wait until you come. There is no negation in the English and there is none in the meaning; the `ne` is simply part of how Croatian builds *until*. The contrast is the lesson's sharpest item: *Čekat ću **dok dođeš*** would mean *I'll wait while you come*, which nobody wants to say. And `dok ne` takes a **perfective present**, because *until* points at a completed arrival.
  4. **`ako` versus `kad`, decided on certainty alone.** *Ako **bude kiše**, nećemo ići na vrh* — it may not rain. *Kad **budeš imao** vremena, javi mi* — you will have time, we just don't know when. English blurs this constantly; Croatian does not. And `ako` takes the same two forms as everything else: perfective present or Futur II.
  5. ⚠️ **`ako` still cannot carry a question**, and that has not changed since Unit 21. *\*Pitao me ako imam vremena* ❌ → *Pitao me **imam li** vremena* ✅. One English word, two Croatian jobs, and this unit is the second half of the pair.
  → grammar-reference **§20** + §19 (the `ako` ≠ question rule) + §11

The long `dok` is therefore built on *čekati*, which is carded, imperfective, and is the unit's canonical Futur II verb: *Dok budem čekao Marka…*

### L4 — Hrvatski velikani (Time clauses in biography)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new machinery. The lesson runs the whole system through **biography**, which is where time clauses live most naturally, and it does one new thing: it puts the system **into both directions of time at once**.
  1. **Past biography takes past time clauses** and behaves entirely normally — *Kad je bio mlad, živio je u Beču* → **CORRECTION: Beč is uncarded** → *Kad je bio mlad, živio je u Zagrebu.* This is L1's `kad`, and it is here to be contrasted.
  2. **Future biography — a plan, a project, an anniversary — takes the unit's rule**: *Kad **dođeš** u galeriju, vidjet ćeš njegovo prvo djelo.*
  3. **The two side by side in one paragraph** is the lesson's real content, because that is what a museum label or a guidebook actually does: it tells you what happened and then tells you what you will see.
  4. **The aspect fork in the wild.** Biography is full of stretches (*pisao je dvadeset godina*) and events (*napisao je jednu knjigu*), and the same verbs reappear in the future clauses, where the fork now decides the tense. Drill the pairs.
  → grammar-reference **§20** + §11 + §8
- **New vocab:** L4 set (artists, writers, works)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — a life as time clauses** (born → studied → wrote → died → and now, when you visit, you'll see) · a past ‖ future two-column table using the same verbs in both · **callout — the same verb, two clauses, two tenses** (*pisati*: *dok je pisao* ‖ *dok bude pisao*) · a biography-vocabulary table
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L2 ban ×1 + L3 dok ne ×1 + L1 aspect ×1) → Hook: "A life is a stack of time clauses. So is a plan. The only difference is which way they point." → vocab-cards (L4 set) → Teach 1: past biography with past `kad` → Teach 2: the same paragraph pointed forward + the same-verb callout → Examples + TTS: *Kad je bio mlad, živio je u Zagrebu.* · *Dok je pisao, radio je i kao učitelj.* · *Napisao je svoje prvo djelo u dvadesetoj godini.* · *Kad dođeš u galeriju, vidjet ćeš njegove slike.* · *Čim uđeš, naći ćeš** → **CORRECTION: naći is rejected** → *Čim uđeš, vidjet ćeš njegov kip.* → quiz-set ×4 (past or future clause? which tense?) → match (life event ↔ its clause) → fill ×3 → type ×2 (turn a past biography sentence into a visitor's future one) → listen-type ×2 → speak (describe one person's life in three time clauses) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** L1's past narration, L2's ban, L3's four conjunctions, U19's `koji` (biography needs relative clauses: *pisac koji je živio ovdje*)
- **SRS:** L4 vocab; cloze "Kad ___ u galeriju, vidjet ćeš njegove slike. (doći, ti)" → dođeš; cloze "Dok je ___ , radio je i kao učitelj. (pisati)" → pisao; rule card "past clause → ___ · future clause → ___" → past tense / present or Futur II
- **Note:** every historical claim in this lesson is generic — *a writer*, *a painter*, *he* — and no real named person is used. The unit is a grammar unit and the biography is scaffolding; inventing plausible facts about a real Croatian figure is a cost with no teaching benefit.

### L5 — Moja kulturna ruta (Consolidation + the writing task)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new machinery. The lesson sorts the system, names **the one licensed exception**, and walks the wall.
  **The card, one last time:**
  > **Main clause → Futur I.** **Coupling → one of four conjunctions.** **Time clause → the PRESENT if the verb is perfective, FUTUR II if it is imperfective — and the aspect chooses, not you.**

  | veznik | primjer | što traži |
  |---|---|---|
  | **kad** | Kad dođem, javit ću ti. | prezent · svršeno |
  | **ako** | Ako budeš imao vremena, idemo u galeriju. | Futur II · nesvršeno |
  | **čim** | Čim stignem, kupit ću ulaznicu. | prezent · svršeno |
  | **dok** | Dok budem čekao, čitat ću knjigu. | Futur II · nesvršeno |
  | **dok ne** | Čekat ću dok ne dođeš. | prezent + a `ne` that means nothing |

  **AND THE ONE EXCEPTION, WHICH IS NOT AN EXCEPTION AT ALL.** *Pitao me **kad ću doći**.* ✅ — because that `kad` is **Unit 21's question word**, not this unit's conjunction. The clause is not naming the time of anything; the clause **is** the thing being asked. Lands one unit after U21 taught embedded questions, and the timing is the whole point.
  > **The test is never the word. It is what the clause is doing.**
  > names the time of the main event → temporal → prezent / Futur II
  > is the thing being asked, or not known → question → Futur I

  **THE WALL OF TEN TRAPS** (§20.6): **(1)** Futur I inside the time clause — the unit's #1 error, and the first thing the rubric checks · **(2)** *budem* used for a perfective verb (*kad budem došao*) — **heavy, not wrong**, and marked as such · **(3)** the present used for an imperfective (*\*dok čekam, čitat ću*) — which reports something happening now · **(4)** *dok* where *dok ne* belongs · **(5)** the `ne` of *dok ne* translated as a negation · **(6)** `ako` used where `kad` belongs, and vice versa, on certainty · **(7)** `ako` carrying a question (U21's error, still live) · **(8)** the l-participle not agreeing (*\*budem radila* said by a man) · **(9)** Futur I removed from the **main** clause too (*\*Kad dođem, javim ti* — a real sentence, but it is a promise, not a plan) · **(10)** a comparative or a participial passive reaching in from B2.
- **New vocab:** L5 set (culture, tradition, exhibitions)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — the two-clause sorter** (which clause am I in? → time clause → what aspect? → the tense) · **the veznik table above, as the unit's exit printable** · **callout — the one licensed Futur I** (with the U21 cross-reference) · **the wall of ten traps** · the writing scaffold (route / arrival / first stop / condition / while-clause / closing) with a model phrase in each cell
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (one item from each of L1–L4) → Hook: "One card, four conjunctions, and one sentence that breaks every rule on it — legally." → vocab-cards (L5 set) → Teach 1: the sorter + the veznik table → Teach 2: the licensed Futur I (the exception, placed **after** the rule and never before) → Teach 3: the wall of ten → mixed quiz-set ×4 → match (conjunction ↔ demand) → fill ×4 → reorder ×2 → type ×2 → listen-type ×2 → speak (a 30-second route plan) → the scaffold slide + the model text → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U21's embedded questions (the exception depends on them entirely), U19's `koji`, U9's Futur I, U17's aspect
- **SRS:** L5 vocab; cloze "Kad ___ u Hrvatskoj, putovat ću tjedan dana. (biti, ja)" → budem; cloze "Pitao me kad ___ doći." → ću; rule card "temporal kad → ___ · interrogative kad → ___" → present or Futur II / Futur I
- **Note:** the exception is taught **last**, after four lessons of the ban, and the notes page says explicitly that meeting it earlier would have undone the rule. The notes page for L5 IS the pre-writing checklist.

## Story — «Dvorac koji nas je čekao» (The castle that was waiting for us)

**~320 words (B1 length: 300–500).** Saturday, a day trip, and a plan in which every single arrangement is a time clause.

**Ana** has the plan and states it in the unit's grammar: *Kad stignemo, kupit ćemo ulaznice. Čim vidimo dvorac, idemo u kulu.* **Tom** watches the sky and adds the conditions: *Ako bude kiše, nećemo ići na vrh.* **Baka Marija** asks the one question that takes a Futur I — *pitala je kad ćemo krenuti* — which is the unit's licensed exception arriving in the middle of a story that has banned it four times. **Marko** oversleeps; the bus leaves without him; he arrives at noon. And then the plan breaks, in the one sentence that had a `čim` in it: the tower is closed, and Marko quotes Ana's own clause back at her verbatim so the reader can check that nothing about the grammar was wrong. **Ana** rebuilds the plan in the same grammar she used to make it. **Baka Marija** closes with the generic `kad` — *Kad se dođe u dvorac, uvijek nešto ostane za drugi put* — which is the impersonal `se` from U20 and the third mode of `kad`, and the title lands sideways.

Cast: Tom, Ana, Marko, baka Marija.
New glossed words (**4**, all verified absent from the 888-headword dump): **vrh** (m, the top — GEN vrha, *na vrhu kule*), **stube** (f pl, steps / stairs — plural only, like *vrata* and *novine*), **kraljica** (f, queen — beside L1's carded *kralj*), **zatvoreno** (CLOSED — quoted as the word **on** the sign, an object in the world, never as something a character says).
*(Struck during the collision pass: **ulaz** = CARDED a2u10 L2 — used freely in the prose instead, which is a gift, because the sign has to hang somewhere and «Na ulazu su bile stube» needs no gloss. **natpis** was already glossed in **b1u20's** story and needs no second gloss — it is used freely here. **kino / trg / muzej / autobus / oblak / nebo** all carded.)*
Sample sentences: *Kad stignemo, kupit ćemo ulaznice.* · *Čim vidimo dvorac, idemo u kulu.* · *Ako bude kiše, nećemo ići na vrh.* · *Dok budemo čekali Marka, razgledat ćemo tvrđavu.* · *Pitala je kad ćemo krenuti.* · *Kad se dođe u dvorac, uvijek nešto ostane za drugi put.*
**Authoring guards:** every time clause in the story is annotated in a code comment with **its conjunction, its tense and the aspect reason** — and if a tense cannot be justified from the verb's aspect, the sentence is wrong. **Exactly ONE Futur I appears after any kad / ako / čim / dok in the whole file**, it is baka Marija's embedded question, and it is marked ★ in the header as the licensed exception; every other `ću / ćeš / će / ćemo / ćete` in the story stands in a main clause after a comma. **No comparatives or superlatives** (B2) — the castle wants to be *the oldest* and must not be; *pet stotina godina* does that work. **No participial passive** (B2) — the tower is not *zatvorena*; the word `ZATVORENO` appears **only** as the text on the sign, which is the licence U20's own sign wall established. **No reported speech with backshift** (U21's rule still applies, and the story reports three times). **No -nje** (U23) · **never three clitics in one clause** (U23). Reasons take **jer** or U20's **budući da**. No `nitko`, `naći`, `točno`, `sjediti`, `stajati`, `kasniti`, `probuditi se`, `gore`, `dolje`, `ovamo`, `stizati`, `zid` (all zero-occurrence or previously rejected).
Comprehension: 5 MC questions (*Što će Ana kupiti kad stignu?* · *Što će biti ako bude kiše?* · *Zašto Marko nije došao autobusom u devet?* · *Što je baka radila kad je bila mlada?* · *Zašto nisu išli na vrh kule?*) — each answerable only by reading a time clause correctly. The third is rewritten from the spec's original *«Zašto Marko kasni?»* because **`kasniti` has zero occurrences**; the story says instead that he slept until nine and the bus left without him.

## Writing task (B1 unit shape)

- **Title:** Moja kulturna ruta (My cultural route)
- **Task (EN):** Write 80–150 words in Croatian planning a cultural route through Croatia. Say **when you arrive** what you will do (*Kad stignem…*), **what you will do as soon as** something happens (*Čim…*), **what you will do while** something else is going on (*Dok budem…*), **one thing that depends on a condition** (*Ako…*), and **one thing you will not do until** something happens (*…dok ne…*). Use at least one **perfective present** time clause and at least one **Futur II**. Give one reason with *jer* or *budući da*. Do **not** put a Futur I inside any time clause. Use the L5 scaffold.
- **Word range:** 80–150 (counter shown)
- **Required elements (the rubric checks all five):** ≥1 time clause with a **perfective present** · ≥1 time clause with **Futur II**, the participle agreeing · ≥1 condition with **ako** · ≥1 **dok** or **dok ne** clause · one reason with *jer* or *budući da*
- **Model text (HR, ~115 words, revealable):** authored in the content file — every time clause annotated in a code comment with its conjunction, its tense and its aspect reason; only taught vocabulary; all five required elements present and identifiable
- **Rubric focus:** **the ban first** — is there a Futur I inside any time clause? That is the error the unit exists to prevent and is marked hardest, with the one exception explicitly carved out: an embedded question (*pitala me kad ću doći*) is **correct** and should be praised rather than marked. Then **the aspect fork**: a *budem* on a perfective verb is **heavy, not wrong**, and is noted without penalty; a bare present on an imperfective (*\*dok čekam, čitat ću*) **is** wrong and is marked. Then **`dok ne`**: present, and no negation in the meaning. Then **participle agreement** in every Futur II. Then conjunction variety — a paragraph using only `kad` has not shown the system. Finally task completion: all five required elements present.
