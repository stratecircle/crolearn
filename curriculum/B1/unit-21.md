# B1 Unit 21 — Tehnologija (Technology & the internet) · Indirect speech

## Overview

The unit that teaches you to carry somebody else's words. Every reported utterance in this course so far has been a direct quote, and that was deliberate — U18, U19 and U20 each shipped an explicit story guard banning *Rekla je da…* by name, so that indirect speech would arrive here as genuine first contact, with the one fact that decides everything already in place.

**Croatian does not backshift.** English relays *«Dolazim.»* as *she said she **was** coming*, moving the tense one step back so automatically that English speakers do not know they are doing it. Croatian moves nothing at all:

> *«Dolazim.»* → *Rekla je da **dolazi**.*
> *«Bio sam tamo.»* → *Rekao je da **je bio** tamo.*
> *«Doći ću sutra.»* → *Rekla je da **će doći** sutra.*

The learner's whole job in L1 is therefore to **stop doing something**, which is a strange kind of grammar lesson and is taught as one. The good news is that the rule was already planted: U20 L3's *kaže se da…* frames carried exactly this fact, in a construction with nothing else to think about, and L1 opens by cashing that in.

From there the unit walks the three sentence types, because each one changes something different.

**Statements** take `da` + the original tense, and only the **person** moves (*ja* → *on*, *moj* → *njegov*). **Commands** lose the imperative and take `da` + the **present**, person-matched to whoever must act — *«Dođi!»* → *Rekla mi je da **dođem*** — with **neka** + 3rd person for when the person who must act is not in the room. **Questions** split in two: a wh-question keeps its question word and simply stops being a question (*Pitala me **gdje stanujem***, no inversion, no question mark), while a yes/no question takes **li** on the verb (*Pitala me **stanujem li** u Zagrebu*).

And the English speaker's instinct at that last point — reaching for **ako**, because English says *if* — is **the headline error of the entire unit** and is killed on sight in L3. `ako` is Croatian's *if-then*, a conditional conjunction, and it cannot carry a question. One English word, two Croatian jobs, and they are not interchangeable.

L4 opens the network of verbs that take a `da`-clause — *obećati*, *priznati*, *spomenuti*, *predložiti*, *obavijestiti*, *pristati* beside the already-owned *reći*, *misliti*, *znati*, *čuti*, *objasniti* — and reuses U19's clitic rule unchanged: inside a `da`-clause, **`da` is slot one**. L5 sorts all three types back together and hands the learner a real conversation to report.

Technology carries the unit because relaying is what a phone is for. Every message, every screenshot, every group chat is somebody's words in somebody else's mouth, and the story is a message that survives four relays and arrives wrong: two people at the wrong place, one an hour early, and nobody lied at any point. Unit shape: 5 lessons + 300–500-word story + unit test + guided writing task (80–150 words, AI rubric-graded, never gates).

## Can-do objectives

By the end of Unit 21 the learner can:

1. **Report a statement without moving its tense**: *«Dolazim.»* → *Rekla je da dolazi.* · *«Bio sam tamo.»* → *Rekao je da je bio tamo.*
2. **Report a command or a request**, matching the person who has to act: *«Dođi!»* → *Rekla mi je da dođem.* · *«Neka Marko dođe.»* → *Rekla je neka Marko dođe.*
3. **Report a wh-question**, keeping the question word and dropping the question: *«Gdje stanuješ?»* → *Pitala me gdje stanujem.*
4. **Report a yes/no question with `li` on the verb**, and never with `ako`: *«Stanuješ li u Zagrebu?»* → *Pitala me stanujem li u Zagrebu.*
5. **Use a network of reporting verbs** — obećati, priznati, predložiti, spomenuti, obavijestiti, pristati — with `da` in second-position order.
6. **Write 80–150 words** reporting a conversation or a message exchange, with at least one statement, one command and one question relayed correctly.

## Lessons

### L1 — Moj mobitel (Reported statements — the tense does not move)

- **Grammar micro-point:** reported statements, and one fact that governs the whole unit.
  1. **The frame:** *reći* (or *misliti*, *znati*, *čuti*, *objasniti* — all already owned) **+ da +** a complete clause. *Rekla je **da dolazi**.* The clitic goes into **second position of the da-clause**, with `da` counting as slot one — U19's rule, unchanged, and U20's frames ran it once already: *Rekla je da **je** to problem.*
  2. **THE RULE: Croatian does not backshift.** Whatever tense the speaker used, that tense stays. *«Dolazim.»* → *Rekla je da **dolazi**.* (English: *she said she **was** coming*.) *«Bio sam tamo.»* → *Rekao je da **je bio** tamo.* *«Doći ću.»* → *Rekla je da **će doći**.* The test that always works: **delete the frame and check that a normal Croatian sentence in its original tense is left over.** U20 L3 taught exactly this test with *kaže se da…*, so this is a callback, not an install — and saying so out loud is worth five minutes.
  3. **What DOES change: the person.** Pronouns and possessives re-point, exactly as in English. *«**Ja** sam **svoj** mobitel spremio.»* → *Rekao je da **je svoj** mobitel spremio.* / *…da je **njegov** mobitel spremljen* → **CORRECTION: participial passive is B2** → *Rekao je da je spremio **svoj** mobitel.* And time words re-point too, but that is L5's problem, not L1's.
  4. **Negation:** *Rekla je da **ne** dolazi.* — the negation stays inside the reported clause, where it was. *Nije rekla da dolazi* means something else and both are taught.
  → grammar-reference **§19 "Indirect speech"** (new) + §12 clitic order + §9 (the tenses that stay put)

### L2 — Poruka za tebe (Reported commands and requests)

- **Grammar micro-point:** what happens to an **imperative** when you relay it. It cannot survive — Croatian has no reported imperative — so it is replaced, and by what depends on who has to act.
  1. **The person who must act is IN the conversation → `da` + the PRESENT**, person-matched to them: *«Dođi!»* → *Rekla mi je **da dođem**.* (she told **me**, so **I** am the one coming → *dođem*) · *«Požurite!»* → *Rekao nam je **da požurimo**.* The tense is **present**, never an imperative and never a future, and the person is whoever has to do it — which is usually **not** the speaker.
  2. **The person told goes in the DATIVE:** *Rekla **mi** je…*, *Rekao **nam** je…*, *Rekla **Marku** je…* → **CORRECTION: clitic order** → *Rekla je **Marku**…* The dative is what makes the person-match unambiguous, and it is also the clitic that has to be placed correctly (§12).
  3. **The person who must act is NOT in the room → `neka` + the 3rd person present:** *«Neka Marko dođe.»* / *Rekla je **neka Marko dođe**.* `neka` is a genuine third-person imperative — Croatian's only way to give an order to somebody absent — and it takes **no `da`**.
  4. **Requests soften the same way.** *«Molim te, provjeri to.»* → *Molila me **da to provjerim**.* The verb *moliti* is what the learner has been saying since A1 as *molim*, now with its own grammar.
  5. **What is NOT this:** *odbiti* takes an **infinitive**, not a `da`-clause — *Odbio je doći* — and is carded here precisely so the learner meets one verb that does not join the pattern before L4 generalizes it.
  → grammar-reference **§19** + §10 (imperative) + §12

### L3 — Pitao me… (Reported questions — `li`, and never `ako`)

- **Grammar micro-point:** questions, which split in two and are the unit's hardest lesson.
  1. **A wh-question keeps its question word and stops being a question.** *«Gdje stanuješ?»* → *Pitala me **gdje stanujem**.* No inversion, no question mark, no `da`. The question word — *gdje, kad, tko, što, kako, zašto, koji* — does the joining all by itself. The only things that change are the **person** and nothing else, because the tense still does not move.
  2. **A yes/no question takes `li` on the verb.** *«Stanuješ li u Zagrebu?»* → *Pitala me **stanujem li** u Zagrebu.* The verb comes first inside the embedded clause and `li` clips onto it. The learner has known `li` since A1 as the question particle (*Je li to istina?*); this is the same particle doing a new job.
  3. **⚠️ THE HEADLINE ERROR OF THE UNIT: `ako` cannot carry a question.** English uses *if* for both — *if it rains* and *she asked if I live in Zagreb* — and Croatian splits them absolutely. `ako` is the **conditional** conjunction and only ever means *if-then*. \**Pitala me **ako** stanujem u Zagrebu* ❌ is not a small slip; it is a different sentence, and a Croatian listener hears a conditional clause dangling with nothing to condition. **The repair is always `li` on the verb.** This gets its own slide, its own callout, its own drill block and two test items.
  4. **`da li` is also out.** It exists, it is heard, it is common in Serbian and in speech — and this course does not produce it. `li` on the verb is the form to write. (Consistent with the course-wide `da li` ban, which every unit spec has carried since A2.)
  5. **The softener:** *Zanima me…* + an embedded question — *Zanima me stanuješ li u Zagrebu* / *Zanima me gdje stanuješ* — which is the polite way to ask an indirect question in Croatian and is worth more socially than it looks.
  → grammar-reference **§19** + §15 (question formation) + §12

- **Required visuals:** **bracket diagram — two kinds of question** (top: "what kind of question was it?" → a **wh-question** → keep the question word, drop the question ‖ a **yes/no question** → `li` on the verb) · **callout — ako is not «if»** (the unit's most important warning slide: two English sentences with *if*, two completely different Croatian ones, and the ❌ crossed out in red) · a wh-word table (gdje / kad / tko / što / kako / zašto, each with a quote and its report) · a `li`-placement train (verb → li → the rest)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L1 no-backshift ×1 + L2 person-match ×1 + A1 `Je li…?` recognition ×1) → Hook: "English uses one word — *if* — for two completely different things. Croatian has never confused them, and today is the day you stop." → vocab-cards (L3 set) → Teach 1: wh-questions keep their word and lose the question (the bracket, left half) → Teach 2: yes/no questions take `li` on the verb (the bracket, right half + the `li` train) → **Teach 3: the `ako` callout** — the unit's headline error, on its own slide → Examples + TTS: *«Gdje stanuješ?»* → *Pitala me gdje stanujem.* · *«Kad dolaziš?»* → *Pitao me kad dolazim.* · *«Stanuješ li u Zagrebu?»* → *Pitala me stanujem li u Zagrebu.* · *«Imaš li lozinku?»* → *Pitao me imam li lozinku.* · *Zanima me je li to istina.* · \**Pitala me ako imam vremena* ❌ → *Pitala me **imam li** vremena* ✅ → quiz-set ×4 (two `li`-vs-`ako` items, one wh-item, one word-order item) → match (question ↔ its report) → fill ×3 (*«Imaš li mobitel?» → Pitala me ___ ___ mobitel.* → imam li) → type ×2 (report one wh-question, one yes/no) → listen-type ×2 → speak (relay three questions somebody asked you) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** A1/§15's `Je li…?` (the same particle, a new job), L1's no-backshift in every item, L2's person-match, U19's `koji` in one example only
- **SRS:** L3 vocab; cloze "«Gdje stanuješ?» → Pitala me ___ ___ ." → gdje stanujem; cloze "«Imaš li lozinku?» → Pitao me ___ ___ lozinku." → imam li; rule card "a reported yes/no question takes ___, never ___" → li on the verb / ako; cloze "Zanima me ___ ___ to istina." → je li
- **Note:** the `ako` error is not a vocabulary problem and it will not be fixed by being mentioned once. It gets a teach slide, a callout, two quiz-set items, one fill and one test item — six separate contacts — because it is the single most durable error English speakers make in Croatian indirect speech.

### L4 — Internet i mi (The da-clause verb network)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the network of verbs that take a `da`-clause, and the two things that vary across it.
  1. **The already-owned core:** *reći*, *misliti*, *znati*, *čuti*, *objasniti*, *smatrati*, *tvrditi*, *vjerovati*, *sumnjati* — nine verbs the learner has been carding since A2, every one of which takes `da` + a clause. Naming them as a set is most of the lesson.
  2. **The new ones, and what each demands:** *obećati **da** + future* (a promise is about later) · *priznati **da** + any tense* · *spomenuti **da**…* · *obavijestiti **nekoga da**…* (+ ACC of the person) · *primijetiti **da**…* · *upozoriti **nekoga da**…* (L2's verb, now in the network) · *ispričati* (which takes a whole story, not a `da`-clause: *ispričao je što se dogodilo*).
  3. **The one that behaves like a command:** *predložiti **da** + the **PRESENT***, exactly like L2's reported imperative, because a suggestion is a soft order — *Predložio je **da dođemo** u pet.* Not a future. This is the trap in the set.
  4. **The ones that take an INFINITIVE instead, and therefore never join:** *odbiti* (L2), *pristati na* + ACC, *moći*, *morati*, *htjeti*. Naming the non-members is what stops the pattern from over-generalizing.
  5. **The clitic rule, one last time:** inside a `da`-clause, **`da` is slot one**, so the clitic lands immediately after it — *Obećao je da **će** doći*, *Priznao je da **ga** nije vidio*. Two clitics maximum at B1; the full cluster is U23.
  → grammar-reference **§19** + §12 + §6
- **New vocab:** L4 set (promising, admitting, suggesting)
- **Required visuals:** **usage-map diagram — what does each verb demand?** (three boxes: **da + any tense** → reći, misliti, znati, priznati, spomenuti, primijetiti ‖ **da + the PRESENT** → predložiti ‖ **an infinitive, never da** → odbiti, pristati, moći, morati) · **callout — predložiti takes the present** (the set's one trap) · the full verb-network table (verb · what follows · example) · a clitic-placement strip for `da`-clauses
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L3 `li` vs `ako` ×2 + L2 person-match ×1) → Hook: "You have been collecting these verbs for two levels without knowing they were a set. Here is the set — and the two members that break it." → vocab-cards (L4 set) → Teach 1: the network + what each demands (the usage map + the table) → Teach 2: *predložiti* + present, and the infinitive-takers that never join → Examples + TTS: *Obećao je da će doći.* · *Priznala je da nije provjerila poruku.* · *Predložio je da dođemo u pet.* · *Spomenuo je da ima novi mobitel.* · *Obavijestili su me da je aplikacija spremna.* → **CORRECTION: participial passive** → *Obavijestili su me da aplikacija radi.* · *Primijetio sam da je zaslon prljav.* · *Odbio je doći.* · *Pristao je na to.* → quiz-set ×4 (pick what follows each verb; one item is *predložiti*) → match (verb ↔ what it demands) → fill ×3 (*Obećao je ___ ___ doći.* → da će) → type ×2 → listen-type ×2 → speak (report three things using three different verbs) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U19's opinion frames (*mislim da*, *smatram da* — the same network, met a unit early), U20's *kaže se da* (the ownerless version of exactly this), L1's no-backshift, U9's future inside a `da`-clause
- **SRS:** L4 vocab; cloze "Obećao je ___ ___ doći." → da će; cloze "Predložio je da ___ u pet. (doći, mi)" → dođemo; rule card "predložiti takes da + ___" → the present; cloze "___ je da nije provjerila poruku. (priznati, ona)" → Priznala
- **Note:** the lesson's real value is the **non-members**. A learner who generalizes `da` to every reporting verb will produce \**odbio je da dođe* — which is a real regional form and is not what this course teaches — so *odbiti* and *pristati* are drilled beside the members, not after them.

### L5 — Digitalni život (Consolidation + the writing task)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new machinery. The lesson sorts the three sentence types back together and hands the learner a decision procedure, plus the one thing L1–L4 deliberately postponed.
  **The three-question sorter:**
  1. Was it a **statement**? → `da` + **the original tense**. Only the person moves.
  2. Was it a **command or a request**? → `da` + **the present**, matched to whoever must act — or **`neka`** + 3rd person if they are not there.
  3. Was it a **question**? → a **wh-word** if it had one; **`li` on the verb** if it did not. **Never `ako`. Never `da li`.**
  **And the postponed piece: time and place words re-point.** *«Doći ću **sutra**.»* reported on the day itself becomes *Rekao je da će doći **danas***; *«**Ovdje** sam»* becomes *Rekao je da je **tamo***. This is the one thing indirect speech *does* move in Croatian, and it is held back to L5 on purpose, so that «nothing moves» could be installed cleanly first. The rule is: **tenses never move, deictics always do.**
  **The wall of five traps:** **(a)** backshifting — the unit's #1 error, and the first thing the writing rubric checks; **(b)** `ako` in a reported question — the headline error, and the second; **(c)** `da li` anywhere; **(d)** keeping question word order or a question mark in a reported wh-question (\**Pitala me gdje stanuješ?* ❌); **(e)** forgetting to move the person (\**Rekao je da ja dolazim* when he meant himself).
- **New vocab:** L5 set (the -ost nouns + digitalan)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — the three-question sorter** (the unit's exit printable) · **callout — the wall of five traps** · a deictic-shift table (sutra → danas/sljedeći dan · ovdje → tamo · ovaj → taj · sada → onda) · the writing scaffold (opening / one statement / one command / one question / one reaction / closing) with a model phrase in each cell
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (one item from each of L1–L4) → Hook: "Three questions, and the first one is: what kind of sentence was it before you touched it?" → vocab-cards (L5 set) → Teach 1: the three-question sorter → Teach 2: **tenses never move, deictics always do** + the wall of five traps → mixed quiz-set ×4 (each item is a different sentence type and the learner must identify which before answering) → match (type ↔ its rule) → fill ×4 (one per type + one deictic) → reorder ×2 (a reported yes/no question with `li`; a reported command with a dative) → type ×2 (report a two-line exchange) → listen-type ×2 → speak (report a 30-second conversation) → the scaffold slide + the model text → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U19's `koji` (the model text carries two relative clauses, because reporting who said what needs them), U20's `kaže se da` (the ownerless relay, contrasted with the named one), U12 clitics, U9 future
- **SRS:** L5 vocab; cloze "«Doći ću sutra.» (reported the next day) → Rekao je da će doći ___ ." → danas; cloze "«Imaš li mobitel?» → Pitala me ___ ___ mobitel." → imam li; rule card "tenses ___ move, deictics ___ move" → never / always
- **Note:** the notes page for L5 IS the pre-writing checklist and must be written as one — six rows, a model text, and the trap wall.

## Story — «Poruka koja je stigla četiri puta» (The message that arrived four times)

**~360 words (B1 length: 300–500).** Ana sends one message about Saturday. It reaches everybody, and it reaches nobody intact.

**Ana** writes to Tom: *«Vidimo se u subotu u pet ispred kina.»* **Tom**, relaying it to Marko by phone, reports it correctly but adds a hedge he was not given — *Rekla je da se vidimo u pet, ali mislim da je mislila na šest* — and the hedge is what survives. **Marko**, passing it to baka Marija, reports Tom's hedge as Ana's statement. **Baka Marija**, who does not use the group chat, asks the one question nobody thought to ask — *Pitala je gdje se nalazi ta kavana* — and gets a wh-question relayed back at her as a yes/no one. By Saturday, Tom is at the cinema at five, Marko is there at six, Ana is at the kavana she thought everybody had agreed on, and baka Marija is at a different cinema entirely, on time.

Nobody lied at any point, and the story is built so the reader can trace exactly where each relay lost something. The closing beat is Ana reading her own original message aloud — the only direct quote in the last paragraph — and the family discovering that it said precisely what it said.

The story is built so that **each paragraph carries a different construction**, in the order the lessons taught them: a reported **statement** in Tom's relay, a reported **command** in Marko's, a reported **question** in baka Marija's, the L4 **verb network** in the group's mutual accusations (*obećao si*, *priznaj*, *spomenuo si*, *predložila je*), and the L5 **deictic shift** in the final paragraph, where *«u subotu»* has become *«danas»* and everybody notices at once.

Cast: Tom, Ana, Marko, baka Marija.
New glossed words (**4**, all verified absent from the 848-headword dump): **nalaziti se** (impf, to be located — *Gdje se nalazi kavana?*), **dogovor** (m, an arrangement / what was agreed), **usput** (adv, by the way / in passing), **kavana** (f, a café — the place, beside the long-carded drink *kava*).
*(Struck during the collision pass: **grupa** = CARDED b1u19 L4 · **kino** = CARDED a1u5 L2 · **jasno** = CARDED b1u19 L3 — all three are used freely in the prose instead. **zbunjen** was struck as a participial-passive shape (B2). **točno** (1 use) and **naći** (1 use) are both non-zero only inside a previous unit's own rejection notice and stay rejected — which forces Ana's original message to be built on the carded **vidjeti se**: «Vidimo se u subotu u pet ispred kina.», not «Nađemo se…».)*
Sample sentences: *Rekla je da se vidimo u pet.* · *Rekao mi je da ponesem mobitel.* · *Pitala je gdje se nalazi kino.* · *Pitala me imam li njezin broj.* · *Obećao si da ćeš provjeriti poruku.* · *Predložila je da dođemo u pet.* · *Rekao je da će doći danas.*
**Authoring guards:** every reported clause in the story is annotated in a code comment with **its type** (statement / command / question), **its tense before and after** (which must be identical), and **the person shift** applied — if a tense differs between the quote and the report, the sentence is wrong. **No `ako` in any reported question** and **no `da li` anywhere** — the story's whole plot depends on a question being relayed correctly, so a violation here would be self-defeating. **No backshifting.** **No `budem` / Future II** (U22). **No participial passive** (B2) — no *zbunjen*, *dogovoreno*, *napisano* as predicates. **No comparatives or superlatives** (B2). **No verbal nouns in -nje** (U23). **No full clitic clusters** — two clitics maximum, and `da` is never counted as one. Reasons take **jer** or U20's **budući da** — no `zbog`, no `zato što`. No `čiji`, no `ono što` / `sve što`. No `ekran`, `zaboraviti`, `šutjeti`, `navika`, `telefon`, `naći`, `točno`, `nitko`, `cesta`, `gasiti`, `parkirati`, `također`, `drugačije`, `uredan` (all zero-occurrence or previously rejected). **mobitel / poruka / aplikacija / lozinka / mreža / internet / poslati / nazvati / odgovoriti / objaviti / dijeliti / pratiti / stranica** are all carded (a2u12, a2u15) and free; **razgovor** and **javiti** are uncarded but live and are permitted.
Comprehension: 5 MC questions (*Što je Ana napisala u poruci?* · *Što je Tom dodao?* · *Što je baka Marija pitala?* · *Tko je došao u šest?* · *Zašto su svi bili na drugom mjestu?*) — each answerable only by tracking **which relay changed what**, so the three sentence types are all doing the comprehension work. The fourth question is answerable only by noticing that Tom's hedge became Marko's statement.

## Unit test blueprint (20 items + writing, pass ≥ 70% on quiz sections)

1. **Vocab (5):** match ×1 (4 pairs: zaslon / tipkovnica / korisnik / podatak ↔ one-line descriptions of what each thing IS, never translations), MC ×2 (*preuzeti* ‖ *spremiti* — which one brings it from the internet? ‖ *odbiti* ‖ *pristati* — which one is «no»?), fill ×2 (*Moram ___ novu aplikaciju.* → instalirati; *___ me je li to istina.* → Zanima)
2. **Grammar (8):** no-backshift MC ×2 (*«Bio sam tamo.» → Rekao je da ___ tamo.* je bio / je bio bio / bude ‖ *«Doći ću.» → Rekla je da ___ .* će doći / dolazi / došla je), reported-command fill ×1 (*«Dođi!» → Rekla mi je da ___ .* → dođem), **`li` vs `ako` MC ×2** (*Pitala me ___ imam vremena.* ako / li — with `li` needing to move onto the verb ‖ *Pitao me ___ ___ u Zagrebu.* stanujem li / ako stanujem — and the explanation must say that `ako` is the CONDITIONAL conjunction and cannot carry a question), wh-question fill ×1 (*«Gdje stanuješ?» → Pitala me ___ ___ .* → gdje stanujem), *predložiti* MC ×1 (*Predložio je da ___ u pet.* dođemo / ćemo doći — the present, like a command), reorder ×1 (*Pitala me stanujem li u Zagrebu.*)
3. **Listening (4):** listen-type ×2 (*Rekla mi je da dođem u pet.* · *Pitao me imam li lozinku.*), listen + MC ×2 (hear *Rekla je da dolazi.* → "is she coming now or was she coming then?" — and the answer is that Croatian does not tell you, because the tense never moved ‖ hear *Rekla je neka Marko dođe.* → "who is being told?" → Marko, who is not in the conversation)
4. **Čitanje (3):** a 7-line group-chat transcript in which one message is relayed twice and changes once, with taught vocabulary only; 3 MC hinging on **who originally said what**, **which relay introduced the error**, and **which sentence is a question rather than a statement**
5. **Writing task (attached to the test, AI-graded, never gates):** see below.
Example item: MC — *Pitala me ___ imam vremena.* (ako / li) → **li**, and it moves onto the verb: *Pitala me **imam li** vremena.* `ako` is the conditional conjunction — it means *if-then* and cannot carry a question · listen + MC — TTS: *Rekla je da dolazi.* "Is she coming now, or was she coming then?" → Croatian does not say. The tense inside `da` is the tense she used, and it never moved, so the sentence is neutral between the two.

## Writing task (B1 unit shape)

- **Title:** Razgovor koji sam prenio (A conversation I passed on)
- **Task (EN):** Write 80–150 words in Croatian reporting a conversation or a message exchange — real or invented. You must relay **at least one statement** (*Rekao je da…*), **at least one command or request** (*Rekla mi je da…* / *Neka…*), and **at least one question** (*Pitao me gdje…* / *Pitala me … li …*). Say what you did about it, and give one reason with *jer* or *budući da*. Do **not** move any tense, and do **not** use *ako* to report a question. Use the L5 scaffold.
- **Word range:** 80–150 (counter shown)
- **Required elements (the rubric checks all five):** ≥1 reported statement with its original tense intact · ≥1 reported command or request, person-matched · ≥1 reported question, using a wh-word or `li` · ≥1 verb from the L4 network other than *reći* · one reason with *jer* or *budući da*
- **Model text (HR, ~110 words, revealable):** authored in the content file — every reported clause annotated in a code comment with its type, its unchanged tense, and its person shift; only taught vocabulary; all five required elements present and identifiable
- **Rubric focus:** **the tense above all** — for every reported clause, is the tense the one the original speaker used? A backshift is the error this unit exists to prevent and is marked hardest. Then **`ako` in a reported question**, which is marked second-hardest and always with the repair shown (`li` on the verb). Then `da li` anywhere. Then the person-match in reported commands, and the dative of the person told. Then the clitic in second position of the `da`-clause, counting `da` as slot one. Then deictics: *sutra* / *ovdje* / *ovaj* re-pointed if the report is made at a different time or place — noted but weighted lightly, since it is L5 material and depends on a context the learner sets themselves. Finally task completion: all five required elements present.
