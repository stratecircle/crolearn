# B1 Unit 20 — Okoliš (Environment) · The se-passive & impersonal constructions

## Overview

The unit that teaches the learner to take the person out of the sentence. Everything said in this course so far has had somebody doing it — *ja radim*, *Ana kupuje*, *oni recikliraju* — and that is a problem, because an enormous amount of what adults actually say has no doer in it at all. *Plastic is recycled here.* *You can't park there.* *People say it's getting warmer.* *One should do something.* English solves this three separate ways — a passive, a generic *you*, an indefinite *people/one* — and Croatian solves all three with **one word the learner has owned since Unit 1 and has never once understood**: `se`.

The whole unit rests on a single fork, and it is a fork about **whether there is a thing in the sentence**:

> **If a thing is there, the thing is the subject, and the verb agrees with it.** *Ovdje se **reciklira plastika**.* · *Ovdje se **recikliraju boce**.*
> **If no thing is there, nothing is the subject, and the verb freezes in the 3rd person singular.** *Ovdje se **ne baca smeće**.* · *Kako se **živi** u Hrvatskoj?*

Nearly every error in this unit is the same error: leaving the verb in the singular when a plural thing is sitting right there in the sentence. The unit is built to drill exactly that agreement, and L1 and L2 are deliberately two halves of one lesson, split so the contrast has somewhere to happen.

Three things make this teachable now and not earlier. First, `se` has been in the learner's mouth since *zovem se* and has been quietly accumulating ever since — *sjećati se*, *nadati se*, *sviđati se*, *slagati se* — so the unit opens by naming the species the learner already owns (the **lexical** `se`, welded to its verb and meaningless on its own) and then introducing the two new ones beside it. Second, `se` is a **clitic**, so §12's second-position rule from Unit 12 governs it completely and nothing new has to be learned about where it goes. Third, U19's relative clauses give the learner somewhere to put all of this: *grad u kojem se dobro živi*, *problem o kojem se govori*.

L3 adds the construction that lets you report a claim without owning it — *kaže se da…*, *smatra se da…*, *zna se da…* — which is the one honest way to say "apparently" before Unit 21 hands over reported speech proper. L4 goes back for `trebati`, which the learner has been using personally since A2 (*trebam pomoć*) and which has an impersonal twin that means something quite different (*treba pomoći*), plus the U18 conditional's polite form *trebalo bi*. L5 consolidates and writes.

The environment carries the unit because environmental language is almost entirely agentless — nobody signs their name to pollution, and every rule about rubbish is a rule about what *is done*, not about who does it. The story is a neighbourhood arguing about a bin. Unit shape: 5 lessons + 300–500-word story + unit test + guided writing task (80–150 words, AI rubric-graded, never gates).

## Can-do objectives

By the end of Unit 20 the learner can:

1. **Say what is done somewhere without naming who does it**, with the verb correctly agreeing with the thing: *Ovdje se reciklira plastika.* / *Ovdje se recikliraju boce i papir.*
2. **State a rule or a general truth about a place** with no subject at all: *Ovdje se ne baca smeće.* · *U Hrvatskoj se puno hoda.* · *Ne smije se bacati staklo u tu kantu.*
3. **Report a shared claim without taking responsibility for it**: *Kaže se da je klima sve toplija.* → **CORRECTION: comparatives are B2** → *Kaže se da je zagađenje veliki problem.* · *Smatra se da recikliranje pomaže.* · *Zna se da je plastika štetna.*
4. **Distinguish the impersonal from the personal `trebati`**: *Treba štedjeti energiju* (one should) ‖ *Trebam pomoć* (I need) ‖ *Trebalo bi nešto učiniti* (something ought to be done).
5. **Tell the three `se`s apart on sight** — lexical (*zovem se*), passive (*reciklira se*), impersonal (*živi se*) — and know that a verb which already owns a lexical `se` cannot take a second one.
6. **Write 80–150 words** describing how the environment is looked after where they live, in the agentless register, with the verb agreeing correctly throughout.

## Lessons

### L1 — Priroda oko nas (The se-passive with things)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the **se-passive**, and the one rule that makes it work. A sentence with a doer — *Ljudi ovdje recikliraju plastiku* — can drop the doer entirely by adding `se` and promoting the object to subject: *Ovdje **se reciklira plastika**.* Three consequences, and the second is the whole unit:
  1. **`se` is a clitic**, so §12 governs it exactly: second position, counted from the start of the clause. *Ovdje **se** reciklira plastika.* · *Plastika **se** reciklira ovdje.* · *U ovom gradu **se** reciklira plastika.* Never \**Se reciklira plastika* ❌ — a clitic cannot open a sentence, and the learner has known this since Unit 12.
  2. **The thing becomes the SUBJECT, so the verb agrees with it.** This is the error the entire unit exists to prevent. *Ovdje se **reciklira plastika**.* (sg) ‖ *Ovdje se **recikliraju boce**.* (pl) ‖ *Ovdje se **recikliraju novine**.* (pl — and *novine* is plural-only, so the singular is never available). An English speaker hears "is recycled" and freezes the verb; Croatian does not. Drill it with the plural first and the singular second, so the plural is never the surprise.
  3. **No doer can be added.** English can say *the house is being built by workers*; Croatian's `se`-passive cannot take an agent at all. \**Kuća se gradi od radnika* ❌. If you want to name the doer, you use the ordinary active sentence you already have: *Radnici grade kuću.* The construction exists precisely for when the doer is unknown, irrelevant, or everybody.
  Say out loud that this is the **second** species of `se` the learner has met, and put it beside the first: *zovem se* is **lexical** — that `se` belongs to the verb, cannot be removed, and means nothing on its own. This new one is grammatical and does a job. → grammar-reference **§18 "The se-constructions"** (new) + §12 clitic order

- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — dropping the doer** (three boxes: [Ljudi recikliraju plastiku.] → the doer is deleted and `se` takes its place → the object moves up into the subject slot → [Plastika se reciklira.]) · **callout — THE AGREEMENT RULE** (the unit's central printable: "the thing is the subject → the verb agrees with the thing", with *plastika se reciklira* / *boce se recikliraju* set one above the other and the endings coloured) · a two-species `se` table (lexical *zovem se, sjećam se, sviđa mi se* ‖ passive *reciklira se, bacaju se*) with a "can you delete it?" column · agreement table (m sg / f sg / n sg / plural × one verb)

The lesson's job is to make the agreement automatic while there is always something visible to agree with.

### L2 — Kako se živi u Hrvatskoj (The impersonal se)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the **impersonal se** — the same word, the opposite situation. Take away the thing and there is nothing left to be the subject, so the verb has nothing to agree with and **freezes in the 3rd person singular**: *Ovdje **se ne baca smeće**.* · *Kako **se živi** u Hrvatskoj?* · *U Zagrebu **se puno hoda**.* · *Odavde **se lijepo vidi** grad.* Four things to teach:
  1. **The frozen form.** The verb is always 3sg, and in the past it is always the **neuter singular** l-participle: *Prije se **govorilo** drukčije* → **CORRECTION: drukčije is 0** → *Prije se **živjelo** mirno.* → **CORRECTION: mirno is b1u19 story-only, uncarded** → *Prije se **živjelo** dobro.* This is the one place in Croatian where a past tense has no gender to agree with, and it looks wrong to a learner who has spent two levels making participles agree.
  2. **The three English translations, one Croatian sentence.** *Ovdje se ne baca smeće* is simultaneously *rubbish is not thrown here*, *you can't throw rubbish here*, and *one doesn't throw rubbish here*. Give all three and refuse to pick a favourite; the learner's job is to stop translating and start recognizing the shape.
  3. **The modal stack**, which is what makes this immediately useful: *može se*, *ne može se*, *mora se*, *ne smije se*, *treba se* — modal in 3sg, `se` in second position, infinitive after. *Ovdje **se ne smije** parkirati.* → **CORRECTION: parkirati is 0** → *Ovdje **se ne smije** bacati staklo.* · *Kako **se može** doći do centra?* `smjeti` was carded back at a2u11 L2 and this is the construction it was waiting for.
  4. **The hard limit, and it is the lesson's best five minutes:** a verb that already owns a **lexical** `se` cannot take a second one. *Kako se osjećaš?* has a `se` already; *how does one feel?* cannot be \**Kako se se osjeća* ❌ and cannot be \**Kako se osjeća* ❌ either (that means *how does he feel*). Croatian repairs it by putting a real subject back in: *Kako se **čovjek** osjeća?* So: **one `se` per clause, ever.** This also retro-explains why *zovem se* could never have been passive. → grammar-reference **§18 "The se-constructions"** + §12
- **New vocab:** L2 set (rules, cleanliness, sorting)
- **Required visuals:** **contrast diagram — the unit's spine** (left panel "THERE IS A THING": *Ovdje se recikliraju boce* with the plural verb marked ‖ right panel "THERE IS NO THING": *Ovdje se ne baca smeće* with the frozen 3sg marked — this is the printable the whole unit points back at) · **callout — one `se` per clause** (with *Kako se čovjek osjeća?* as the repair) · modal-stack table (može / ne može / mora / ne smije + se + infinitive) · a "three English sentences, one Croatian sentence" table
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L1 agreement ×2 — one singular, one plural + U12 clitic ×1) → Hook: "Yesterday the verb agreed with the thing. Today there is no thing. Watch what the verb does." → vocab-cards (L2 set) → Teach 1: the frozen 3sg + the neuter past (the contrast diagram) → Teach 2: the modal stack + the one-`se`-per-clause callout → Examples + TTS: *Ovdje se ne baca smeće.* · *U Hrvatskoj se puno hoda.* · *Kako se živi u tom gradu?* · *Ovdje se ne smije bacati staklo.* · *Kako se može doći do parka?* · *Prije se živjelo dobro.* · *Kako se čovjek osjeća kad je sam?* → quiz-set ×4 (frozen or agreeing? — four sentences, two of each, and the learner must decide which species it is before choosing the verb) → match (Croatian impersonal ↔ its three English readings, 4 pairs) → fill ×3 (*Ovdje se ne ___ bacati otpad.* → smije) → type ×2 (write a rule for a place, using ne smije se) → listen-type ×2 (*Ovdje se ne baca smeće.* · *Kako se živi u Hrvatskoj?*) → speak (three rules where you live) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** L1's agreement rule appears in **every** quiz-set item as the thing being contrasted against, U12 clitic position, a2u11 `smjeti` (its first real workout since it was carded), U19 `koji` in one example (*grad u kojem se dobro živi*)
- **SRS:** L2 vocab; cloze "Ovdje se ne ___ smeće. (bacati)" → baca; cloze "Kako se ___ u Hrvatskoj? (živjeti)" → živi; cloze "Prije se ___ dobro. (živjeti, past)" → živjelo; rule card "no thing in the sentence → the verb freezes in the ___" → 3rd person singular (neuter in the past)

### L3 — Problemi planeta (Kaže se / smatra se / zna se da…)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the impersonal `se` applied to **verbs of saying and thinking**, which produces the construction for reporting a claim you are not vouching for. *Kaže **se da**…* (it is said that / apparently) · *Smatra **se da**…* (it is held that) · *Zna **se da**…* (it is known that) · *Misli **se da**…* · *Vjeruje **se da**… ·* The frame is: **3sg verb + se + da + an ordinary complete clause.** Four points:
  1. **`da` is obligatory**, exactly as it was in U19's opinion frames, and the clitic cluster goes into second position **of the da-clause**, counting `da` as slot one: *Kaže se da **je** zagađenje veliki problem.*
  2. **The tense inside the `da`-clause does not move.** Whatever the claim was, that is the tense: *Kaže se da je bilo toplo.* This is a quiet, deliberate preview of the single most important fact in Unit 21 — Croatian never backshifts — planted here where it costs nothing and pays off there.
  3. **The register difference is worth naming.** U19 taught *Mislim da…* (I think), which puts the speaker's name on it. *Smatra se da…* takes the name off. The pair is the unit's whole theme in miniature, and L3's examples deliberately re-run U19 sentences with the owner removed.
  4. **These are frames, not reported speech.** There is no named speaker anywhere, and *Rekao je da…* does not appear in this unit at all — that is U21's first lesson and the story guard protects it.
  → grammar-reference **§18** + §12 (clitics inside a da-clause) + §17 (the da-frame from U19)
- **New vocab:** L3 set (planet, climate, pollution, consequences)
- **Required visuals:** **usage-map diagram — who is on the hook?** (three boxes: "I am" → *Mislim da…* (U19) ‖ "everybody / nobody in particular" → *Smatra se da…* ‖ "it is simply true" → *Zna se da…*, each with an example) · **callout — the tense does not move** (with *Kaže se da je bilo toplo* worked, and a forward-pointer to U21) · frames table (kaže se / smatra se / zna se / misli se / vjeruje se, with English and a strength column) · a cause→consequence flow for the vocabulary (zagađenje → posljedica → utjecaj)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L2 frozen-vs-agreeing ×2 + U19 `da`-clause clitic ×1) → Hook: "There is a way to say something out loud without being the one who said it." → vocab-cards (L3 set) → Teach 1: the frame + obligatory `da` + the clitic in second position of the da-clause → Teach 2: the tense does not move + the who-is-on-the-hook usage map → Examples + TTS: *Kaže se da je zagađenje veliki problem.* · *Smatra se da recikliranje pomaže.* · *Zna se da je plastika štetna.* · *Misli se da je klima problem cijelog svijeta.* · *Kaže se da je prije bilo čišće.* → **CORRECTION: comparative** → *Kaže se da je prije bilo drugačije.* → **CORRECTION: drugačije is 0** → *Kaže se da je prije bilo dobro.* → quiz-set ×4 (insert `da`; place the clitic; pick the frame that matches the strength of the claim) → match (frame ↔ English) → fill ×3 (*Smatra se ___ je to opasno.* → da) → type ×2 (take a *Mislim da…* sentence and take the owner off it) → listen-type ×2 (*Kaže se da je zagađenje veliki problem.* · *Zna se da je plastika štetna.*) → speak (three claims about the environment, each with a different frame) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U19's `da`-frame and its clitic rule (this lesson is that lesson with the subject removed, and the parallel is drawn explicitly), L2's frozen 3sg (these frames ARE the frozen 3sg — the lesson must not present them as new machinery, only as a new use), U17 aspect in the da-clause
- **SRS:** L3 vocab; cloze "Kaže ___ da je to problem." → se; cloze "Smatra se ___ je recikliranje korisno." → da; rule card "kaže se da + ___" → the original tense, unchanged
- **Note:** the temptation here is to teach *apparently* as a word. There isn't one at B1; there is a frame. Every example must keep the `da`-clause fully grammatical on its own, so the learner sees that nothing inside it changed.

### L4 — Što možemo učiniti? (Impersonal treba + infinitive vs personal trebam)

- **Grammar micro-point:** `trebati`, which the learner has been using since A2 and has been using **only one of its two ways**. The fork:
  1. **Personal `trebati` + a NOUN in the accusative** = *to need*. The person is the subject and the verb agrees with them: *Trebam pomoć.* · *Trebaš odmor.* · *Trebamo više vremena.* → **CORRECTION: comparative** → *Trebamo vremena.* This is the one the learner already owns.
  2. **Impersonal `treba` + an INFINITIVE** = *one should / it is necessary to*. The verb is **frozen in 3sg**, exactly like L2, and there is no subject at all: *Treba štedjeti energiju.* · *Treba odvojiti staklo od papira.* · *Ne treba trošiti struju.* Note there is **no `se`** in this one, which is why it needs its own lesson — it is impersonal without being a se-construction, and the learner who has spent three lessons adding `se` to everything will add one here.
  3. **The polite third form:** *trebalo bi* + infinitive = *one ought to / something should be done* — the U18 conditional, in the neuter singular, doing exactly what L2's frozen past did. *Trebalo bi nešto učiniti.* This is the softest way to make a demand in Croatian and is worth its weight for the writing task.
  4. **The error to name and kill:** *trebam ići* — personal subject with an infinitive — is extremely common in speech and is **not** what a B1 learner should be producing, because it collapses the two constructions the lesson just separated. The lesson's line is: if there is a noun, use the personal one; if there is an infinitive, use `treba` or `trebalo bi`. Say that the other thing exists and that it is not being taught yet.
  Contrast with the already-owned `morati` (a2u11): *moram* is a hard obligation with a named person; *treba* is a soft, ownerless one. → grammar-reference **§18** + §10 (conditional) + §6
- **New vocab:** L4 set (energy, saving, reducing)
- **Required visuals:** **bracket diagram — one verb, two doors** (top: "what follows it?" → a NOUN → personal *trebam/trebaš/treba* + ACC ‖ an INFINITIVE → impersonal *treba* frozen, or *trebalo bi*) · **callout — no `se` here** (the trap slide: *Treba štedjeti energiju* ✅ ‖ \**Treba se štedjeti energiju* ❌) · a strength table (mora se ‖ treba ‖ trebalo bi ‖ može se, ordered hardest to softest, each with the same sentence) · the full *trebati* present beside the frozen *treba*
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L2 modal stack ×1 + L3 frame ×1 + L1 agreement ×1) → Hook: "You have known this verb since A2 and you have been using half of it." → vocab-cards (L4 set) → Teach 1: the two doors (the bracket) + the ACC after the personal one → Teach 2: *trebalo bi* + the strength table + the no-`se` callout → Examples + TTS: *Trebam pomoć.* · *Treba štedjeti energiju.* · *Treba ugasiti svjetlo kad izlaziš iz sobe.* → **CORRECTION: svjetlo (0) and izlaziti (0)** → *Treba ugasiti struju kad nikoga nema.* · *Ne treba trošiti vodu.* · *Trebalo bi smanjiti otpad.* · *Trebamo novu kantu.* → quiz-set ×4 (noun or infinitive? pick trebam / treba) → match (four sentences ↔ hardest-to-softest) → fill ×3 (*___ štedjeti vodu.* → Treba) → type ×2 (soften a *mora se* sentence into a *trebalo bi* one) → listen-type ×2 (*Treba štedjeti energiju.* · *Trebalo bi nešto učiniti.*) → speak (three things one should do, three things you personally need) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U18's conditional (*trebalo bi* is its most useful single form and this is the first time it has had a real job), a2u11 `morati` and `smjeti` (the strength table is a review of both), L2's frozen 3sg, U17 aspect on the infinitive (*štedjeti* vs *uštedjeti* is mentioned once and not drilled)
- **SRS:** L4 vocab; cloze "___ štedjeti energiju. (impersonal)" → Treba; cloze "___ pomoć. (I need)" → Trebam; cloze "___ ___ nešto učiniti. (one ought to)" → Trebalo bi; rule card "noun after it → ___ ‖ infinitive after it → ___" → personal trebam + ACC / frozen treba
- **Note:** this lesson is the unit's only lesson without a `se` in its grammar point, and that is deliberate — it is placed here so the learner meets an impersonal construction that is NOT a se-construction while the se-constructions are still fresh, which is the only way to stop `se` from becoming a reflex.

### L5 — Zeleni grad (Consolidation + the writing task)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new machinery. The lesson sorts all four constructions back together and gives the learner a decision procedure and a scaffold. The sorting question, in order:
  1. Is there a **thing** the sentence is about? → se-passive, and **the verb agrees with it** (L1).
  2. Is there **no thing at all**? → impersonal se, verb frozen 3sg, neuter in the past (L2).
  3. Is it a **claim somebody-or-other makes**? → *kaže se / smatra se / zna se* **da** + the original tense (L3).
  4. Is it **advice or a requirement**? → *treba* / *trebalo bi* + infinitive, and **no `se`** (L4).
  Then the four standing traps, gathered on one wall: **(a)** the verb left in the singular when the thing is plural — the unit's #1 error, and the first thing the writing rubric looks for; **(b)** `se` opening a sentence; **(c)** two `se`s in one clause, including the invisible kind where the verb already owns one; **(d)** a `se` inserted after `treba`. Plus **(e)**, which is the B2 neighbour and the one the learner will reach for unprompted: writing *zagađeno je* / *zabranjeno je* — the **participial passive**, which is real Croatian and is not this unit and is not taught until B2. When the learner wants it, the repair is always one of the four constructions above.
- **New vocab:** L5 set (protection, solutions, change)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — the four-question sorter** (the decision procedure above as a four-step flow, the unit's exit printable) · **callout — the wall of five traps** · the writing scaffold table (opening / what is done / what is not allowed / what people say / what should be done / closing) with a model phrase in each cell · a `se`-species reference strip (lexical ‖ passive ‖ impersonal) for the notes page
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (one item from each of L1–L4) → Hook: "Four constructions, one question: is there a thing in this sentence?" → vocab-cards (L5 set) → Teach 1: the four-question sorter → Teach 2: the wall of five traps → mixed quiz-set ×4 (each item is a different construction and the learner must identify which before answering) → match (construction ↔ its diagnostic question) → fill ×4 (one per construction) → reorder ×2 (a se-passive with a plural subject; a *ne smije se* rule) → type ×2 (translate two sentences that force different constructions) → listen-type ×2 → speak (describe how rubbish is handled where you live, 30 seconds) → the scaffold slide + the model text → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U19 `koji` (the writing task's model text carries two relative clauses, because "the town I live in" is the natural opening), U18 conditional (*trebalo bi*), U17 aspect, U12 clitics
- **SRS:** L5 vocab; cloze "U mom gradu se ___ otpad. (odvajati)" → odvaja; cloze "Boce se ___ u taj spremnik. (bacati)" → bacaju; rule card "is there a thing? → ___ ‖ no thing? → ___" → the verb agrees / the verb freezes
- **Note:** the notes page for L5 IS the pre-writing checklist and must be written as one. The learner should be able to open it, read six rows, and write the paragraph.

## Story — «Kanta ispred zgrade» (The bin in front of the building)

**~350 words (B1 length: 300–500).** A new communal container appears on the pavement in front of the building and nobody knows what is supposed to go in it. **Ana** reads the sign on the lid out loud — the sign is written entirely in the impersonal register, because signs always are — and discovers it says one thing while the neighbours have been doing another. **Marko** has been putting everything in it for two weeks and defends himself in the only way available: *Ja nisam znao. Tu se uvijek sve bacalo.* **Tom**, who is new to the country, asks the question the whole unit is an answer to — *Kako se to ovdje radi?* — and gets four different answers from four people, which is the joke. **Baka Marija** closes it by not arguing about the bin at all: she remembers when there was no bin, when there was nothing to throw away, and when a jar was a jar for thirty years. Her last line lands the title sideways.

The story is built so that **each paragraph carries a different construction**, in the order the lessons taught them: the se-passive with agreeing plural subjects in the sign Ana reads, the impersonal se in Marko's defence and Tom's question, the *kaže se / smatra se* frames in the neighbours' contradictory answers, and *treba / trebalo bi* in the resolution. Baka Marija's closing paragraph uses the impersonal **past** — *prije se živjelo drugačije* → **CORRECTION: drugačije is 0** → *prije se živjelo mirno* → **CORRECTION: mirno is uncarded** → *prije se nije bacalo ništa* — which is the only place in the unit the neuter past appears in prose, and it is placed last so it reads as a closing cadence rather than a rule.

Cast: Tom, Ana, Marko, baka Marija.
New glossed words (≤5, all verified absent from the 808-headword dump): **poklopac** (m, lid; GEN **poklopca** — fleeting a), **staklenka** (f, jar), **natpis** (m, a sign / an inscription — the words written on the lid), **pločnik** (m, the pavement — *na pločniku ispred zgrade*), **stanar** (m, a resident of the building; pl stanari). *(**zgrada** = a1u5 L4 and **susjed** = a2u10 L1 are both CARDED and were struck from this list during the collision pass — they are used freely in the prose instead, which is a gift: the bin has to stand in front of something and the neighbours have to be called something.)*
Sample sentences: *Na poklopcu se vidi natpis.* · *Ovdje se bacaju samo boce i staklenke.* · *Tu se uvijek sve bacalo.* · *Kako se to ovdje radi?* · *Kaže se da spremnik nije za papir.* · *Trebalo bi pitati susjede.* · *Prije se nije bacalo ništa.*
**Authoring guards:** every `se` in the story is annotated in a code comment with its species — **lexical**, **passive** (and then the subject it agrees with, named) or **impersonal** (and then "frozen 3sg" or "frozen neuter past") — and if a species cannot be justified, the sentence is wrong. **No participial passive** anywhere: no *zagađen*, *zabranjeno*, *otvoreno*, *napisano* as predicates — the sign says *Ovdje se bacaju…*, never *Zabranjeno je…*, and this is the guard most likely to be violated because a real Croatian sign would say exactly that. **No two `se`s in one clause**, and no impersonal `se` on a verb that owns a lexical one. **No reported speech** (U21): the neighbours speak in **direct quotes**, and second-hand claims take the L3 frames, never *Rekao je da…*. **No `budem` / Future II** (U22). **No comparatives or superlatives** (B2) — not *najveći*, not *sve više*, not *čišće*. **No verbal nouns in -nje** beyond the two carded plain nouns. Reasons take **jer** or L5's **budući da** — no `zbog`, no `zato što`. No `čiji`, no `ono što` / `sve što`. No `cesta`, `gasiti`, `okolina`, `rasti`, `drugačije`, `drukčije`, `mirno`, `svjetlo`, `parkirati`, `također`, `izlaziti`, `pušiti`, `prodavati`, `trajati`, `dio`, `pamtiti`, `pogriješiti`, `nitko` (all zero-occurrence, or non-zero only inside a previous unit's own rejection notice — `nitko` was rejected by name in b1u19's test header and stays rejected). `tako` is carded (b1u19 L5) and permitted; `znati`, `dugo`, `nešto`, `ništa`, `problem` are uncarded but in live use and permitted; `kažem / kaže` is established receptively by b1u17 L3, which is exactly what makes L3's `kaže se` frame legal. **smeće** (a2u10 L3), **čistiti** (a2u10 L3), **susjed** (a2u10 L1), **zajedno** (a2u12 L1), **smjeti** (a2u11 L2) are carded and free.
Comprehension: 5 MC questions (*Što se baca u novi spremnik?* · *Što je Marko radio dva tjedna?* · *Što Tom pita?* · *Što bi trebalo učiniti?* · *Čega se baka Marija sjeća?*) — each answerable only by reading a construction correctly, and the first is answerable **only** by noticing that the sign's verb is plural, which makes the agreement rule do the comprehension work.

## Writing task (B1 unit shape)

- **Title:** Kako se u mom gradu čuva okoliš (How the environment is looked after in my town)
- **Task (EN):** Write 80–150 words in Croatian about how the environment is looked after where you live. Say **what is done** (*Kod nas se…*), **what is not allowed** (*Ne smije se…*), **what people say about it** (*Kaže se da…* / *Smatra se da…*), and **what should be done** (*Trebalo bi…*). Use at least one **plural** subject in a se-passive, so the agreement is visible. Use the L5 scaffold.
- **Word range:** 80–150 (counter shown)
- **Required elements (the rubric checks all five):** ≥2 se-passives, **at least one with a plural subject** · ≥1 impersonal se with no subject at all · ≥1 *kaže se / smatra se / zna se da* frame · ≥1 *treba* or *trebalo bi* + infinitive · one reason with *jer* or *budući da*
- **Model text (HR, ~110 words, revealable):** authored in the content file — every `se` species-labelled in a code comment, every se-passive's subject named, only taught vocabulary, all five required elements present and identifiable
- **Rubric focus:** **the agreement above all** — in every se-passive, does the verb match the thing, and is the plural actually plural? Then the species discipline: exactly one `se` per clause, never sentence-initial, never after `treba`. Then the frames: `da` present, the tense inside it unmoved. Then the absence of the participial passive — a *zabranjeno je* or *zagađeno je* is not marked wrong as Croatian, but is marked as the wrong unit, with the repair shown. Finally task completion — all five required elements present.
