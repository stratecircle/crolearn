# B1 Unit 19 — Mišljenja (Opinions & debate) · Relative clauses: koji through all cases

## Overview

The unit that lets the learner stop speaking in short sentences. Until now every idea has needed its own clause — *Imam prijatelja. On radi u restoranu.* — and the result sounds like a list, not like a person talking. **koji** welds those two clauses into one: *Imam prijatelja **koji** radi u restoranu.* The machinery costs almost nothing new, because `koji` declines exactly like the definite adjective `nov` from Unit 4 — the learner already owns the endings. What has to be installed instead is a single habit of mind, and the whole unit is built to install it: **koji takes its GENDER and NUMBER from the noun it points back at, but its CASE from the job it does inside its own clause.** Those two halves come from two different places, and nearly every learner error in this unit is the same error — importing the case from the antecedent instead of from the relative clause. The opinion theme carries it because relative clauses are where opinions actually live: *tema o kojoj svi govore*, *ljudi s kojima se ne slažem*, *razlog koji nitko ne spominje*. L1 also finally cards the complementizer **da** (restricted to opinion frames), which is what makes *Mislim da…* possible at all, and L3 finally cards **o + LOC**, which U17 and U18 both had to route around. The unit ends with the family arguing about city versus village, and with a written argument paragraph. Unit shape: 5 lessons + 300–500-word story + unit test + guided writing task (80–150 words, AI rubric-graded, never gates).

## Can-do objectives

- I can join two sentences with koji instead of leaving them side by side, and I know that koji can never be dropped the way English drops "that"
- I can pick the right form of koji by asking two separate questions — "what does it point back at?" (gender + number) and "what does it do in its own clause?" (case) — and I know the answers come from different places
- I can put a preposition in FRONT of koji (grad u kojem živim, ljudi s kojima radim) and never leave it stranded at the end the way English does
- I can state, soften and hedge an opinion (Mislim da…, Smatram da…, Čini mi se da…, Nisam siguran, možda…) and give a reason for it with jer
- I can agree, half-agree and disagree politely in a discussion (Slažem se, S jedne strane…, s druge strane…, Iako…, Ipak…) and write an 80–150-word argument paragraph containing at least two relative clauses

## Lessons

### L1 — Mislim da… (Opinion frames + koji in the nominative)

- **Grammar micro-point:** two things, and the second is the unit's foundation.
  1. **The opinion frame:** *Mislim / Smatram / Čini mi se* **+ da +** a normal, complete, present-tense clause. `da` is not optional the way English "that" is — *I think it's a good idea* has to become *Mislim **da** je to dobra ideja.* Word order after `da` is ordinary, and the clitic cluster goes into second position **of the da-clause**, counting `da` as the first slot: *Mislim da **je** to dobra ideja.* / *Mislim da **ga** dobro poznajem.* Negation splits in two and the two mean different things: *Ne mislim da…* (I don't think that…) ‖ *Mislim da ne…* (I think that … not). Teach both.
  2. **koji in the nominative**, i.e. the relative pronoun as the **subject** of its own clause. The central rule in its first, easiest form: *Imam prijatelja **koji** radi u restoranu.* — *prijatelja* is accusative (object of *imam*), but `koji` is **nominative**, because inside its own little clause it is the one doing the working. Gender and number come from *prijatelj*; case comes from the job. Three consequences to say out loud: (a) koji is **never droppable** (\**Imam prijatelja radi u restoranu* ❌); (b) the forms are the definite adjective `nov` with koj- for nov- (§4 → §17), so nothing new has to be memorized; (c) the **comma is restrictive-sensitive** — no comma when the clause says *which one* (*Imam prijatelja koji radi u Zagrebu.*), a comma only when the thing is already identified and the clause just adds information (*Moj brat, koji živi u Splitu, dolazi sutra.*); production defaults to **no comma** and the graders accept both. → grammar-reference **§17 "Relative clauses (koji)"** (new) + §4 definite adjectives + §12 clitic order
- **New vocab:** L1 set (opinion frames + koji-support words)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — welding two sentences** (three boxes: [Imam prijatelja.] + [Prijatelj radi u restoranu.] → the second sentence's subject is replaced by koji → [Imam prijatelja, koji radi u restoranu.]) · **callout — two questions, two answers** (Q1 "what does it point back at?" → gender + number ‖ Q2 "what is its job HERE?" → case, the two arrows drawn coming from opposite directions — this is the unit's central printable and reappears in every lesson) · NOM koji table (koji / koja / koje · pl koji / koje / koja) set beside the `nov` row from §4 so the learner sees it is the same shape · opinion-frame table (Mislim da… ‖ Smatram da… ‖ Čini mi se da… with a "how strong is it?" column)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (U18 conditional clitic ×1 + U4 definite adjective ending ×1 + U12 clitic second position ×1) → Hook: "You have been speaking in short sentences because Croatian has been withholding one word from you. Here it is — and it declines like an adjective you learned in Unit 4, so it is already half yours." → vocab-cards (L1 set) → Teach 1: the da-frame + the clitic in second position **of the da-clause** → Teach 2: the welding flow + the two-questions callout + the NOM koji table → Examples + TTS: *Mislim da je to dobra ideja.* (I think that's a good idea.) · *Smatram da je to istina.* (I hold that that's true.) · *Čini mi se da nije tako.* → **CORRECTION: tako is L5** → *Čini mi se da nemaš pravo.* → **CORRECTION: imati pravo is an untaught idiom** → *Čini mi se da ne razumiješ.* (It seems to me you don't understand.) · *Imam prijatelja koji radi u restoranu.* (I have a friend who works in a restaurant.) · *To je tema koja se svima sviđa.* (That's a topic everyone likes.) → quiz-set ×4 (insert da; pick koji/koja/koje for the antecedent) → match (antecedent ↔ nominative koji-form) → fill ×3 (*Imam prijatelja ___ radi u Zagrebu.* → koji) → type ×2 (weld two given sentences into one) → listen-type ×2 (*Mislim da je to dobra ideja.* · *Imam prijatelja koji radi u restoranu.*) → speak (three opinions with Mislim da…, each with a jer-reason) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U4 definite adjective endings (the whole declension rides on them), U12/U15 clitic second position — now one level down, inside a subordinate clause, U18 conditional appears in **one** warm-up item only, as recognition, never as new production
- **SRS:** L1 vocab; cloze "Mislim ___ je to dobra ideja." → da; cloze "Imam prijatelja ___ radi u Zagrebu." → koji; form card "koji, feminine singular → ___" → koja; rule card "gender + number from ___, case from ___" → the antecedent / its own clause
- **Note:** every koji in this lesson is **nominative and singular** — no exceptions, no previews of the oblique forms. The lesson's entire job is to make the two-questions habit automatic while the second question still always has the same easy answer. L2 is where that answer starts changing.

### L2 — Čovjek kojeg poznajem (koji in the accusative & genitive)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the case now changes, and the animacy split arrives with it.
  - **ACC:** feminine **koju**, neuter **koje**, plural m **koje** / f **koje** / n **koja** — all mechanical. Masculine singular is the one that forks: **animate → kojeg(a)** (= genitive), **inanimate → koji** (= nominative). *Čovjek **kojeg** poznajem.* ‖ *Film **koji** gledam.* This is not a new rule — it is §3's masculine animacy rule, the one that already gives *Vidim Marka* but *Vidim auto*, now applied to a pronoun.
  - **GEN:** **kojeg(a) / koje / kojeg(a)**, plural **kojih** — used after the genitive verbs and quantities the learner already owns: *knjiga **koje** se sjećam* (sjećati se + GEN, U16) · *ljudi **kojih** nema* (nema + GEN, U5).
  - The teach slide must show the trap explicitly: the antecedent's case is **irrelevant**. *Poznajem čovjeka **koji** radi ovdje* (NOM — he is the worker) vs *Ovo je čovjek **kojeg** poznajem* (ACC — he is the known one). Same man, same gender; the only thing that changed is his job inside the relative clause. → grammar-reference §17 + §3 masculine animacy
- **New vocab:** L2 set (knowing, believing, reasons)
- **Required visuals:** **contrast diagram — koji ‖ kojeg** (left: *Čovjek **koji** radi ovdje* — he DOES something, subject, NOM; right: *Čovjek **kojeg** poznajem* — something is done TO him, object, ACC; the same man drawn on both sides) · **usage-map diagram — the masculine singular ACC fork** (central node "m sg ACC" → branch ANIMATE → kojeg (čovjek, prijatelj, gost) → branch INANIMATE → koji (film, grad, stol), each with an example) · **callout — the antecedent's ending is a decoy** (*Poznajem čovjek**a** koj**i** radi ovdje.* — the -a and the -i disagree on purpose; cover the antecedent's ending before you choose) · ACC/GEN rows of the koji table · poznavati vs znati mini-table (poznajem čovjeka / grad ‖ znam odgovor / da je to istina)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L1 nominative koji ×2 + U3 masculine animacy ×1) → Hook: "Same man, two sentences, two different words for «who» — and the difference has nothing to do with him." → vocab-cards (L2 set) → Teach 1: the koji ‖ kojeg contrast + the decoy callout → Teach 2: the animacy fork + the GEN forms → Examples + TTS: *Ovo je čovjek kojeg poznajem.* (This is the man I know.) · *Film koji gledam je dobar.* (The film I'm watching is good.) · *Ne razumijem razlog koji si dao.* (I don't understand the reason you gave.) · *To je knjiga koje se sjećam.* (That's the book I remember.) · *Sumnjam da je to dobra ideja.* (I doubt that's a good idea.) → quiz-set ×4 (animate or inanimate? which form?) → mc ×2 (koji / kojeg for a given antecedent) → match (sentence ↔ the case koji is doing) → fill ×4 (two ACC, one GEN, one NOM distractor from L1) → reorder ×1 (a relative clause with a clitic in it: *Ovo je čovjek kojeg sam vidio jučer.*) → type ×2 → listen-type ×2 (*Ovo je čovjek kojeg poznajem.* · *Film koji gledam je dobar.*) → speak (describe three people you know, using kojeg / koju) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U3 masculine animacy in the accusative (now paying a second dividend), U16 sjećati se + GEN as the genitive host, U8 perfekt inside the relative clause (*kojeg **sam** vidio jučer* — the clitic goes into second position of the **relative** clause, counting kojeg as slot one)
- **SRS:** L2 vocab; cloze "Ovo je čovjek ___ poznajem." → kojeg; cloze "Film ___ gledam je dobar." → koji; contrast card "čovjek koji ___ ‖ čovjek kojeg ___" → radi / poznajem; error card "\*čovjek koji poznajem → ✅ ___" → čovjek kojeg poznajem; form card "poznavati, 1sg → ___" → poznajem
- **Note:** clitic position inside a relative clause is worth its own drill and gets one (the reorder item). `kojeg` occupies the first slot of its own clause, so `sam / je / ga` land immediately after it: *…kojeg **sam** vidio*, never \**…kojeg vidio sam* ❌. Same §12 rule, one level down, and learners consistently miss it.

### L3 — Grad u kojem živim (koji in the locative & dative — prepositions in front)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the lesson where English actively fights the learner.
  - **LOC:** **kojem(u/e) / kojoj / kojem(u/e)**, plural **kojima** — always after a preposition, because the locative has no other life: *grad **u kojem** živim* · *tema **o kojoj** govorimo* · *stol **na kojem** je knjiga*.
  - **DAT:** the same forms, without a preposition, for indirect objects: *prijatelj **kojem** pišem* · *žena **kojoj** sam dao knjigu*.
  - **The habit to break:** English strands its prepositions — *the city I live **in***, *the topic we're talking **about***. Croatian cannot. The preposition marches to the **front**, immediately before koji, and it is the preposition that decides the case: *grad **u kojem** živim*, never \**grad koji živim u* ❌ and never \**grad koji živim* ❌. Teach it as a physical move: find the English preposition, pick it up, put it in front.
  - **o + LOC** is carded here, and is the reason this lesson sits in this unit rather than in a grammar-only one: an opinion is always *about* something. *Mislim o tome.* · *Govorimo o toj temi.* · *To je tema o kojoj svi govore.* → grammar-reference §17 + §14 prepositions by case (o is already listed there under LOC)
- **New vocab:** L3 set (o + the public-topic words)
- **Required visuals:** **flow diagram — moving the preposition** (three frames: [the city I live **in**] → the preposition is lifted out of the end → [grad **u kojem** živim], with the stranded version crossed out) · **callout — the preposition picks the case** (u/na location + koji → LOC kojem/kojoj ‖ o + koji → LOC kojem/kojoj ‖ s + koji → INS, "that one is L4" ‖ u/na motion + koji → ACC koji/koju) · LOC/DAT rows of the koji table · **contrast diagram — u + ACC ‖ u + LOC inside a relative clause** (*grad **u koji** idem* = motion ‖ *grad **u kojem** živim* = location — the U5 rule, still alive, now with koji riding it)
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L2 ACC/GEN koji ×2 + U5 u/na motion-vs-location ×1) → Hook: "«The city I live in.» Croatian will not let you end a sentence like that. The little word has to go to the front — and once it does, it tells you which ending to use." → vocab-cards (L3 set) → Teach 1: the preposition-move flow + the stranding ❌ → Teach 2: LOC/DAT forms + o + LOC + the motion/location contrast → Examples + TTS: *Grad u kojem živim je lijep.* (The city I live in is beautiful.) · *To je tema o kojoj svi govore.* (That's the topic everyone is talking about.) · *Imam prijatelja kojem svaki dan pišem.* (I have a friend I write to every day.) · *Promet je problem o kojem svi govore.* (Traffic is a problem everyone talks about.) · *Nemam jasan stav o toj temi.* (I don't have a clear position on that topic.) → quiz-set ×4 (put the preposition where it belongs; pick kojem/kojoj) → mc ×2 (u koji or u kojem?) → fill ×4 → reorder ×2 (build *grad u kojem živim* and *tema o kojoj govorimo* from scrambled tiles — the tile set includes a trailing preposition as a trap) → type ×2 (translate two stranded English sentences) → listen-type ×2 (*Grad u kojem živim je lijep.* · *To je tema o kojoj svi govore.*) → speak (describe your city and one topic you care about) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U5 u/na motion vs location (the ACC/LOC fork, now with koji), U13 adjectives describing places (*veliki grad*, *lijepa država*), U18 conditional in one opinion sentence (*U gradu u kojem živim, htio bih novi park.*)
- **SRS:** L3 vocab; cloze "Grad ___ ___ živim je lijep." → u kojem; cloze "To je tema ___ ___ svi govore." → o kojoj; error card "\*grad koji živim u → ✅ ___" → grad u kojem živim; rule card "o + ___" → LOC; form card "koji, feminine LOC → ___" → kojoj
- **Note:** `o` is carded here for the first time in the whole course, so it gets a full vocab card of its own with three collocations on it (*govoriti o*, *misliti o*, *knjiga o*), not just a mention inside a grammar slide. Its arrival also retroactively unlocks phrasing that U17 and U18 had to route around — the story and the writing task may now use it freely. Warn once: `o` takes a **noun**, not a clause — \**Mislim o da…* ❌.

### L4 — Ljudi s kojima radim (koji in the instrumental + the full plural)

- **Grammar micro-point:** the last case, and the completed table.
  - **INS:** **kojim / kojom / kojim**, plural **kojima** — after `s/sa` for company (*ljudi **s kojima** radim* · *kolegica **s kojom** pijem kavu*) and bare for means (*vlak **kojim** putujem* — the bare-INS transport rule from §14, so no preposition appears at all).
  - **The plural collapse, taught as a gift:** in the plural, **DAT = LOC = INS = kojima** for all three genders, exactly as *novima* in §4. Three cases, one form. Say so explicitly — it is the single biggest reduction in workload the unit offers.
  - **Plural NOM/ACC** still splits by gender: NOM koji / koje / koja, ACC koje / koje / koja. Drill the collision the learner actually meets: *ljudi **koji** rade* (NOM pl m) vs *ljude **koje** poznajem* (ACC pl m).
  - The **full table** is assembled on screen for the first time here, with the two-questions callout from L1 laid over it. → grammar-reference §17 (full table) + §4 (novima) + §14 (s/sa, bare INS for transport)
- **New vocab:** L4 set (groups, debate nouns)
- **Required visuals:** **the full koji declension table** (6 cases × 4 columns m/f/n/pl, with the m-sg-ACC fork marked and the DAT=LOC=INS **kojima** cells shaded as one block — the unit's second printable) · **bracket diagram — one form, three cases** (kojima at the root, three branches: DAT *ljudima kojima pišem* · LOC *ljudima o kojima govorim* · INS *ljudima s kojima radim*) · **callout — koji ‖ koje in the plural** (subject vs object, the two sentences stacked) · s/sa + INS reminder from §14, with the bare-INS transport exception beside it
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L3 LOC/DAT ×2 + U14 s/sa + INS ×1) → Hook: "Three cases left in the plural — and they are all the same word. Croatian is finally giving something back." → vocab-cards (L4 set) → Teach 1: INS singular + the s/sa collocations + bare INS for transport → Teach 2: the full table + the kojima collapse bracket → Examples + TTS: *Ljudi s kojima radim su dobri.* (The people I work with are good.) · *To je kolegica s kojom pijem kavu.* (That's the colleague I have coffee with.) · *Ovo je generacija koja sve zna.* (This is the generation that knows everything.) · *Ne poznajem ljude o kojima govoriš.* (I don't know the people you're talking about.) · *U grupi u kojoj radim ima pet ljudi.* (In the group I work in there are five people.) → quiz-set ×4 (pick the plural form; identify which of kojima's three jobs is running) → match (case ↔ example sentence, all four using kojima) → fill ×4 → mc ×2 (koji or koje in the plural?) → reorder ×1 → type ×2 → listen-type ×2 (*Ljudi s kojima radim su dobri.* · *Ne poznajem ljude o kojima govoriš.*) → speak (describe the people you work or study with, 3+ sentences, at least two relative clauses) → interleave ×2 → Recap
- **Interleaving:** U14 s/sa + INS for company vs bare INS for transport (this distinction now decides whether a preposition appears in front of koji at all), U4 the *novima* plural that koji copies exactly, U12 kolega/kolegica in their natural sentences
- **SRS:** L4 vocab; cloze "Ljudi ___ ___ radim su dobri." → s kojima; cloze "To je kolegica ___ ___ pijem kavu." → s kojom; rule card "plural DAT = LOC = INS → ___" → kojima; contrast card "ljudi ___ rade ‖ ljude ___ poznajem" → koji / koje
- **Note:** the full table is the moment to re-state the two-questions rule for the fifth time, because from here on **both** questions have hard answers. Every drill item in L4 must be answerable ONLY by asking both questions — no item may be solvable by copying the antecedent's ending, and the item writer must check each one against that standard before it ships.

### L5 — Debata (Agreeing, disagreeing + the writing task)

- **Grammar micro-point:** no new machinery — **all six cases of koji interleaved** under time pressure, plus the discussion register the unit has been building towards. Four moves, taught as a set:
  1. **Agreeing:** *Slažem se (s tobom).* · *Naravno.* · *To je istina.* · *I ja tako mislim.*
  2. **Half-agreeing / conceding:** *S jedne strane…, s druge strane…* · *Iako je to istina, …* · *Ipak, …* · *Možda, ali…*
  3. **Disagreeing politely:** *Ne slažem se.* · *Nisam siguran.* · *Mislim da nije tako.* (Croatian disagreement lands harder than learners expect — the concession comes first.)
  4. **Concluding:** *Zaključak je jasan.* · *Po mome mišljenju…* (taught as a chunk) · *Ja sam za / protiv.*
  Then the unit's exit performance: the guided writing task *"Tema o kojoj imam jasan stav"* (80–150 words), rehearsed on-slide before the test carries it. The scaffold **requires** two relative clauses, so grammar and register are graded together. → grammar-reference §17 (whole section)
- **New vocab:** L5 set (debate register)
- **Required visuals:** **usage-map diagram — four moves in a discussion** (central node "rasprava" → AGREE / HALF-AGREE / DISAGREE / CONCLUDE, each branch with its phrases and a one-line "how it lands" note) · **bracket diagram — s jedne strane… s druge strane** (a balance with prednost on one pan and nedostatak on the other, each pan holding one example sentence) · **callout — writing scaffold** (1 name the topic with *Tema o kojoj…* → 2 state the position with *Mislim da…, jer…* → 3 one advantage with *Prednost je…* → 4 one drawback with *S druge strane, nedostatak je…* → 5 concede with *Iako…* or *Ipak…* → 6 close with *Zaključak je…*) · **the koji table again**, as a narrow reference strip beside the writing rehearsal
- **Slide arc:** menu → warm-up quiz-set (L4 INS/plural ×2 + L1 da-frame ×1) → Hook: "Croatians disagree with each other constantly and stay friends. The trick is four phrases — and the first one agrees with you." → vocab-cards (L5 set) → Teach 1: the four-moves usage map → Teach 2: the two-sides bracket + prednost/nedostatak + the fleeting-a genitives (nedostatka, zaključka) → Examples + TTS: *Slažem se s tobom, ali imam jedno pitanje.* (I agree with you, but I have one question.) · *S jedne strane život u gradu je lakši, s druge strane promet je težak.* (On one hand life in the city is easier, on the other the traffic is hard.) · *Iako je to istina, ipak mislim da nije tako jednostavno.* (Although that's true, I still think it's not that simple.) · *Ja sam protiv toga.* (I'm against that.) · *Zaključak je jasan: svi imaju dobar razlog.* (The conclusion is clear: everyone has a good reason.) → quiz-set ×4 (which move is this? agree / concede / disagree / conclude) → match (phrase ↔ move) → fill ×4 (mixed koji cases, one per case family) → mc ×2 → type ×3 (state an opinion, concede, conclude) → reorder ×1 → listen-type ×2 (*Slažem se s tobom, ali imam jedno pitanje.* · *Iako je to istina, ipak mislim da nije tako.*) → speak (argue one side of «grad ili selo», 4+ sentences, at least two relative clauses) → integration ×2 (the family's table debate — cast) → unit-review quiz-set (6 items sampling L1–L4, one per case) → **writing-rehearsal slide** (the scaffold callout + a ~100-word model text with every koji marked and its case labelled) → Recap + pointer to the test's writing task
- **Interleaving:** U18 conditional in one concession (*Na tvom mjestu bih mislio tako.*), U14 s + INS in *slažem se s tobom*, U16 budućnost in the concluding sentence
- **SRS:** L5 vocab; cloze "___ se s tobom." → Slažem; cloze "S jedne ___, s druge ___." → strane / strane; cloze "___ je to istina, ipak mislim da nije tako." → Iako; form card "GEN of nedostatak → ___" → nedostatka; chunk card "I'm against it → ___" → Ja sam protiv toga
- **Note:** `slagati se` takes **s + INS** for the person (*slažem se **s tobom** / **s Anom***) and `o` + LOC or plain *u tome* for the point — both are taught collocations on the card, not left to guesswork. `protiv` takes **GEN** (*protiv toga*, *protiv te ideje*) and the card must show it, because it is the unit's only new preposition-plus-case pair after `o`. Also warn once: Croatian does not double the concession — \**Iako je to istina, ali mislim…* ❌ → **Iako je to istina, mislim…** ✅.

## Story — «Grad ili selo?» (City or village?)

**~340 words (B1 length: 300–500).** Sunday lunch at baka Marija's. Tom mentions that a friend of his is leaving Zagreb, and the table divides. **Ana** defends the city: *Grad u kojem živim ima sve — posao, kazalište, ljude s kojima mogu raditi.* **Marko** takes the other side from behind the restaurant counter: the guests he likes best are the ones who come in from villages, and the traffic he sits in every morning is the reason he would leave tomorrow. **Tom**, the outsider, half-agrees with both and says so in exactly the L5 register — *S jedne strane…, s druge strane…* **Baka Marija** closes it, as she always does, with the least abstract line in the room: she has lived in both, and what she misses is not the village but the people — *Ne mislim o selu. Mislim o ljudima koje sam poznavala.* The story is built so that **every paragraph carries koji in a different case**, in the same order the lessons taught them: NOM in Ana's opening, ACC and GEN in Marko's, LOC in the traffic paragraph, INS in Tom's, plural in baka's closing. The last line is an opinion, not a resolution — the family does not agree, and that is the point.

Cast: Tom, Ana, Marko, baka Marija.
New glossed words (≤5, all verified absent from the 759-headword dump on 2026-07-29): **mir** (m, peace/quiet), **buka** (f, noise), **gužva** (f, crowd/rush), **miran** (adj, calm/quiet; mirna), **dosadan** (adj, boring; dosadna — fleeting a).
Sample sentences: *Grad u kojem živim ima sve.* · *Ljudi s kojima radim su dobri.* · *Gosti koje najviše volim dolaze sa sela.* · *Promet je razlog o kojem svi govore.* · *Mislim o ljudima koje sam poznavala.* · *S jedne strane mir, s druge strane dosadno.*
**Authoring guards:** every relative clause in the story must survive the two-questions test, and the authoring pass annotates each one with its case in a code comment — if a case cannot be justified from the clause's **own** verb or preposition, the sentence is wrong. No impersonal or passive `se` (U20) — the only `se` permitted is lexical (*slažem se*, *sjećam se*, *sviđa mi se*). No reported speech (U21): characters speak in **direct quotes**, and opinions are framed with *Mislim da…* in the speaker's own voice, never *Rekla je da…*. Reasons take **jer** — no `zbog`, no `zato što` (both zero-occurrence). No `čiji`, no `ono što` / `sve što`. No `tako`, `isto`, `drukčije`, `svatko`, `malen`, `grozan`, `smetati`, `nedostajati` (all zero-occurrence and uncarded; `tako` is carded in L5 and may appear **only** after that lesson). `o + LOC` is now taught and may be used freely. **kazalište** (a1u5 L3), **park** (a1u5 L1), **gost** (a1u8 L3), **mali / velik / lijep** (a1u3) are all carded; **kao**, **cijeli**, **svi**, **sve**, **problem**, **nešto**, **ništa** are uncarded but in live use and are permitted.
Comprehension: 5 MC questions (*Tko brani grad?* · *Zašto bi Marko otišao iz grada?* · *Što Tom misli o gradu i o selu?* · *Gdje je baka Marija živjela?* · *O čemu baka Marija misli?*) — each answerable only by tracking **whose opinion is whose**, so the da-frames and the relative clauses are both doing the comprehension work. The last question is also the unit's only `o + čemu` item and doubles as a §15 callback.

## Unit test blueprint (20 items + writing, pass ≥ 70% on quiz sections)

1. **Vocab (5):** match ×1 (4 pairs: mišljenje / prednost / nedostatak / rasprava), MC ×2 (*poznavati* vs *znati* — which one goes with a person? ‖ which sentence AGREES with the speaker? — Slažem se / Ne slažem se / Nisam siguran / Ja sam protiv), fill ×2 (*Nemam jasan ___ o toj temi.* → stav; *Svaka ideja ima prednosti i ___.* → nedostatke)
2. **Grammar (8):** case-of-koji MC ×3 (*Ovo je čovjek ___ poznajem.* koji/kojeg ‖ *Grad ___ ___ živim je lijep.* u koji / u kojem ‖ *Ljudi ___ ___ radim su dobri.* s kojim / s kojima), animacy MC ×1 (*Film ___ gledam je dobar.* koji/kojeg — and the explanation must say **inanimate**, not "wrong gender"), fill ×2 (*To je tema ___ ___ svi govore.* → o kojoj; *Mislim ___ je to dobra ideja.* → da), preposition-stranding type ×1 (translate *the city I live in* → *grad u kojem živim*), weld-two-sentences type ×1 (*Imam prijatelja. Prijatelj radi u Zagrebu.* → *Imam prijatelja koji radi u Zagrebu.*)
3. **Listening (4):** listen-type ×2 (*Ljudi s kojima radim su dobri.* · *To je tema o kojoj svi govore.*), listen + MC ×2 (hear *Ovo je čovjek kojeg poznajem.* → "does he know the man, or does the man know him?" ‖ hear *Iako je to istina, ipak mislim da nije tako.* → "does the speaker agree?")
4. **Čitanje (3):** a 6-line forum post arguing one side of city-vs-village (taught vocabulary only, four relative clauses in it, one per case family) + 3 MC comprehension hinging on **which noun each koji points back at** and on whether the writer agrees, concedes or objects
5. **Writing task (attached to the test, AI-graded, never gates):** see below.
Example item: MC — *Ovo je čovjek ___ poznajem.* (koji / kojeg) → **kojeg** — he is the OBJECT of *poznajem*, and *čovjek* is animate, so the m-sg accusative borrows the genitive form · listen + MC — TTS: *Iako je to istina, ipak mislim da nije tako.* "Does the speaker agree?" → no: *iako* concedes the point and *ipak* takes it straight back.

## Writing task (B1 unit shape)

- **Title:** Tema o kojoj imam jasan stav (A topic I have a clear opinion about)
- **Task (EN):** Write 80–150 words in Croatian about something you have an opinion on — city or village, work or study, anything from the unit. Name the topic (*Tema o kojoj želim pisati je…*), state your position with a reason (*Mislim da…, jer…*), give **one advantage** and **one drawback** (*S jedne strane… s druge strane…*), concede one point to the other side (*Iako…* / *Ipak…*), and finish with a conclusion (*Zaključak je…*). You must use **at least two relative clauses with koji, in two different cases**. Use the L5 scaffold.
- **Word range:** 80–150 (counter shown)
- **Required elements (the rubric checks all five):** ≥2 relative clauses with koji in two different cases · one *da*-framed opinion with a jer-reason · one advantage and one drawback (prednost / nedostatak, or s jedne strane / s druge strane) · one concession (iako or ipak) · one closing conclusion
- **Model text (HR, ~100 words, revealable):** authored in the content file — every koji form correct and case-labelled in a code comment, only taught vocabulary, all five required elements present and identifiable
- **Rubric focus:** the form of koji above all — gender and number agreeing with the antecedent, case coming from the relative clause (the two-questions rule), and the m-sg animate/inanimate fork. Then preposition placement: in front of koji, never stranded at the end, never dropped. Then the *da*-frame: `da` present and not deleted, clitics in second position of the da-clause. Then discussion register: concession before disagreement, consistent ti or Vi. Finally task completion — all five required elements present.
